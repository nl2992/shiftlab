# G1 screen: uniform filter forgetting and causal-null risk perturbation

**Date:** 2026-10-29. **Status:** derivation completed; no novelty gate passed.

## Candidate claim

Let a finite-horizon learner choose an action (including query and deployment/switch action) from its observable history. The economic law is a known hidden Markov model; the causal-null opponent chooses queried-label probabilities from the full event history, including outcomes still pending. Compare a history policy `pi` with a compressed policy `pibar`. Suppose, at every learner history `h`, their action distributions obey

`TV(pi(.|h), pibar(.| suffix_k(h))) <= epsilon`.

The suffix must include the exact public time, remaining request rights, pending request/release calendar, current deployment, and the last `k` learner-visible observations/actions/releases; only older information is compressed. Let each per-round economic cost be in `[0,cmax]`, and let `R(pi,nu)` be the probability of any promotion against causal-null strategy `nu`.

**Lemma (finite-horizon common-history coupling).** For every fixed full-history causal-null strategy `nu`,

`|R(pi,nu) - R(pibar,nu)| <= min(1,T epsilon)`

and

`|C(pi) - C(pibar)| <= cmax * epsilon * T(T+1)/2`.

Consequently, `|sup_nu R(pi,nu) - sup_nu R(pibar,nu)| <= min(1,T epsilon)`. If `pi` has worst-null risk at most `alpha - T epsilon`, then `pibar` has worst-null risk at most `alpha`.

**Proof.** Couple the two executions using the same economic exogenous randomness and, while their histories agree, the same causal-null randomization. At any common history, maximal coupling makes their actions agree with probability at least `1-epsilon`. Thus the probability that the first action disagreement occurs at round `t` is at most `epsilon`; the probability of any disagreement is at most `T epsilon`. Promotion indicators agree whenever the complete histories agree, proving the risk bound for each fixed `nu`. Taking suprema preserves the absolute-difference bound. If first disagreement occurs at `t`, the remaining total cost difference is at most `(T-t+1)cmax`; summing these worst-case contributions gives the cost bound. The result is finite-horizon and requires no stochastic assumption on the causal-null strategy.

If the economic policy is `L`-Lipschitz in an alternative-model belief under total variation, and a truncated filter satisfies the **uniform pathwise** forgetting condition `TV(b_t, bhat_t) <= C rho^k` for every reachable history, then `epsilon = L C rho^k`. With `rho<1`, choosing `k = O(log(T/target_error))` gives polynomial suffix count for fixed input alphabets, while preserving the causal-null constraint by tightening its target by `T L C rho^k`.

## Assumptions and failure points

The lemma compares policies that are uniformly close at every full history. It does not show that such a compressed policy exists, can be found efficiently, or is near-optimal. The filter condition must hold for every possible sequence of learner actions and delayed label releases, including strings generated by the causal-null opponent; expected or alternative-law-only filter stability is insufficient. The policy must be Lipschitz in the belief, while optimal finite-horizon policies can be discontinuous. A risk margin `alpha - T epsilon >= 0` is also required. Pending queues, query-budget reservations and deployment state remain exact parts of the compressed controller state.

## Registered recurrent-HMM instantiation

The registered two-state alternative uses transition matrix `P=[[3/4,1/4],[1/4,3/4]]` and
queried-label emissions `P(Y=1|S=0)=1/20`, `P(Y=1|S=1)=19/20`; unqueried events provide no
emission update. A one-event filter update is multiplication by either `P` or `D_y P`, where
`D_y` is the positive diagonal emission matrix. Each matrix has projective diameter `log(9)`:
the diagonal emissions cancel from the cross-ratio and the transition cross-ratio is
`(3/4)^2/(1/4)^2=9`. Birkhoff's coefficient is `tanh(log(9)/4)=1/2`. After the first common
suffix event, any two prior beliefs therefore have Hilbert distance at most `log(9)`; each
additional common event contracts this distance by `1/2`. For a suffix of `k>=1` events,

`TV(b_t, bhat_t) <= tanh(log(9) * 2^(-(k-1)) / 4)`.

This bound is pathwise over every queried-label string, not merely high-probability under the
alternative. Here `k` counts HMM event-time filter updates, not raw wall-clock storage. With fixed
delay `d`, the finite-memory implementation must retain at least the last `k+d` observable event
records and the exact pending-label calendar, so it can place each released label at its source
event and propagate the posterior to the current time. Pending outcomes remain hidden until release.
The filter recomputation then uses the same `P` or `D_y P` positive matrices over the retained
event-time window.

For the illustrative binary action rule `P(A=1|b)=sigmoid(b-b*)`, the belief-to-action
Lipschitz constant is `L=1/4`. At `T=6,k=5`, the resulting `epsilon` is `0.008580`, so the
causal-null risk perturbation is at most `0.05148` and leaves `0.04852` of an `alpha=0.1`
constraint for the uncompressed policy. At `T=20,k=8`, the perturbation is at most `0.02146`,
leaving `0.07854`. If each round's loss plus query and switch charges is bounded by `1.11`,
the corresponding absolute cost perturbation bounds are `0.2000` and `0.2501`.

Thus uniform forgetting is not the practical obstruction in this registered HMM: the explicit
bound is nonvacuous at these horizons for a smooth policy. The unresolved obstruction is
algorithmic and statistical: no efficient way to optimize a near-best policy in this restricted
smooth class under the original joint cost/risk contract has been established, and the risk
perturbation remains a direct coupling corollary rather than a novelty claim.

## Novelty audit and decision

This is a useful transfer bound, but the proof is a direct maximal-coupling argument and the policy-compression route is already occupied by finite-memory POMDP approximation. Kara and Yüksel, *Near Optimality of Finite Memory Feedback Policies in POMDPs* (JMLR 2022), construct finite-window policies and give performance-loss bounds under filter stability (Sections 1, 3–4; [primary text](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume23/20-1152/20-1152.pdf)). Kim, *Finite Memory Belief Approximation for Optimal Control in POMDPs* (arXiv:2601.03132, 2026), directly studies finite-memory belief approximation and policy-conditional performance bounds ([primary text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03132)). The added worst-causal-null risk perturbation follows immediately from the same uniform action-kernel coupling; no source-level evidence currently supports calling that extension a sufficiently distinct algorithm or theorem.

There is also no demonstrated complexity improvement over the full causal game: the lemma certifies a supplied approximation but does not optimize it, and generic robust FSC synthesis remains the optimization baseline. Therefore this route does **not** pass G1. Do not implement an efficacy method from it. Keep the lemma as a screening result and seek a different structural source of algorithmic advantage, or stop the proposed methodological direction if no such source survives prior-art review.

The result remains separate from present-regime value: a null-risk bound on historical promotion probability cannot certify a challenger's benefit after a regime reversal.
