A team holds an incumbent and a challenger model, buys delayed labels on disagreements under a hard
budget, and pays a cost to switch. Anytime-valid promotion certificates for this problem are usually
calibrated to a conditional null, which does not control the marginal null on temporally dependent
streams. We study promotion with a declared dependence class and ask what that robustness costs.
We propose \NAD, which tests the challenger against a per-step envelope on the conditional success
probability, spaces label queries by a predictable gap, and reports an anytime
dependence-robustness value. We give a validity argument for the certificate, an
information-rate characterisation of the price of robustness for a relaxed per-step class, a
two-stream design with sign-independent flagging, and a payback-certified variant. On a common
benchmark with author-endpoint comparators, synthetic dependent streams, exact marginal-null twins
