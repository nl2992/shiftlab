# Rollback/acquisition development pilot

## Result class and exact artifacts

This is a development-only synthetic replay, not independent confirmation. It ran 30 paired seeds in
each of three existing regimes (`F3_reversal_800`, `S_reversal_400`, `F4_recurrent`), for 90 paired
episodes per policy and 900 policy rows. All regimes use T=2,000, B=200, delay=100, `c_q=0.05`,
`c_s=5`, and alpha=0.05. The runner returned `done 90 jobs in 66s`.

## Mechanism traces and adverse outcomes

All E_rb runs requested the full 200 labels in all three regimes. Its mean rollback counts were 0.90,
0.27, and 0.33 for F3, F4, and S reversal; mean switches were 1.87, 1.00, and 0.67. Mean first
promotion times were 528.8, 426.9, and 139.3 events respectively. Mean fraction of events on the
wrong model was .536, .646, and .280. The screen records label counts before event 500 and after
event 1000, predictive loss, label cost, switch counts, deadline/budget exhaustion, and the pathwise
wrong-model exposure fields; see the JSONL for complete episode rows.

Key findings and adverse cases:

- The never-switch policy is cheaper than E_rb in every regime here. This small study therefore does
  not support choosing rollback without a prior expectation that a challenger becomes useful and
  later reverses.
- Lowering E_rb rollback alpha to .05 appears helpful in F3 but has no clear gain in S reversal and
  costs more in F4 recurrent. The sign change is incompatible with a universal threshold claim.
- SRR/CUSUM has lower cost than E_rb on F3 but higher cost on F4; SR has lower cost on F3 and F4,
  but its mean switch/rollback counts rise to 2.33/1.17, 4.20/2.10, and 2.00/1.00. Its more frequent
  switching is an adverse deployment pattern and it does not carry RMM's anytime error guarantee.
- E_srr_noacq changes detector and acquisition together, so its mixed cost results cannot isolate
  the value of post-promotion acquisition. B5 also changes reserve, rollback and deadline at once;
  its lower costs on these regimes do not establish the reserve's causal contribution.
- Fixed-reserve B5 promotes much less often than E_rb (means .83 vs .97 on F3, .47 vs .70 on F4,
  .10 vs .33 on S reversal), and its advantage is confounded by lower label expenditure. This is
  consistent with a budget-allocation tradeoff rather than a targeted post-promotion effect.

The report does not classify each rollback as correct or false using evaluator truth; it reports
switch, rollback, and wrong-model exposure summaries. The existing Family B path diagnostics and
ledger remain the source for that separate evaluation. No safety or statistical guarantee is inferred
from this pilot.

## Decision

No distinct joint candidate was identified. The obvious composition, front-loaded acquisition plus
E_rb (with a reserve option), is assembled from existing Family C and Family B mechanisms. This pilot
does not show a stable target-regime advantage over the strongest applicable comparator, never-switch
is cheaper on these episodes, and detector/acquisition contrasts have mixed signs. Close this
development branch without confirmatory evaluation. If work continues, the next defensible step is a
single prospectively frozen question on untouched synthetic and real sources, with pathwise paired
cost accounting and explicit rollback truth classification; it must not treat these pilot outcomes as
independent evidence.
