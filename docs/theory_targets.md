# DIVE theory targets

These are open proof targets. The current implementation establishes none of the unresolved claims by code or simulation.

1. **Sequential validity with covariate-adaptive querying.** State a filtration in which `X_t` is revealed before the query randomization and `Y_t` afterward. Separate the PPI unbiasedness identity conditional on current features from the target null conditional only on prior history. For the registered marginal null, use a bound predictable under the prior-history filtration; normalizing by a bound based on `q_t(X_t)` is invalid without a stronger context-conditional null or an explicit joint-law argument. The exact counterexample and validity scope are in `docs/marginal_null_predictable_bound_audit.md`.
   The independent monitor confirmation compared the established fixed stake-mixture e-process with predictable Kelly using a frozen logistic decision-risk proxy at a matched 20% query rate. On 2,000 streams per null scenario, both processes had 0 alarms in stationary and benign-nuisance conditions; the simultaneous one-sided exact upper bound across four cells was 0.219%. On 1,000 paired seeds per alternative, fixed-mixture monitoring improved macro detection by 13.4 points (95% seed-cluster interval [11.95, 14.90]), with positive scenario differences for boundary harm and harmful adaptation. This confirms a design-specific baseline ranking, not a new process or a theorem about optimality. Residual-variance harm remains undetected and requires a separate estimand/power diagnosis; see `docs/risk_proxy_monitor_confirmation_results.md`.

   **Filtration audit:** for the project's null `E[H_t | F_(t-1)] <= 0`, a current-context propensity `q_t(X_t)` makes the PPI range depend on information outside `F_(t-1)`. The predictable-bound e-process is valid only when its bound is predictable under the null's filtration; choosing it before the label is insufficient. A one-step exact counterexample has `E[H]=0` but `E[1 + 0.5 H/B(X)] = 1.125`. The new [audit](marginal_null_predictable_bound_audit.md) documents the calculation and the test. The 60-seed propensity-floor development run confirms that the invalid eventwise diagnostic can look much more powerful, while the fixed-bound sensitivity explains much of the adaptive-versus-uniform gap. Its eventwise crossings are excluded from evidence. Continue with the fixed-bound marginal estimand or separately register a stronger context-conditional estimand; do not tune the invalid scale.

2. **Delayed feedback and change-time uncertainty.** The `EventOrderBuffer` enforces event-order release and reports pending-label latency. Its tests and the independent-delay grid verify event-time invariance to label-arrival order under outcome-independent delay; this is an implementation check, not a theorem. A fixed equal-weight mixture of eight bounded-mean e-processes with starts fixed before the stream is now a benchmark. Under the conditional-mean null each start-specific process is a supermartingale; the convex mixture is therefore also a supermartingale, and Ville's inequality controls its crossing probability. The `log(8)` cost for allocating prior mass over starts is explicit. The exploratory grid suggests substantial late-harm power gains, but independent null calibration and disjoint alternatives are still required; the construction is established and is not a novelty claim. Still prove validity using the arrival-time filtration, event-time predictable propensities, pending labels at stopping, and selective-label mechanisms; characterize which nonrandom or outcome-dependent delays break the claim. The delayed source/target risk-CS comparator and limits are in `docs/delayed_feedback_protocol.md` and `docs/delayed_feedback_grid_protocol.md`.
3. **Adaptive acquisition value.** Derive an allocation criterion for reducing uncertainty in the decision-harm estimand or probability of a valid escalation, including heteroskedastic residuals, heterogeneous query costs, floors/caps, and a fixed total label budget. Compare it with Neyman allocation and EVSI/EVPI-style acquisition. The standard cost-constrained variance optimum `q ∝ sqrt(v/k)` is implemented and was mixed empirically: it improved stream-estimation RMSE over variance-only querying in five of six synthetic scenarios, but did not improve harm detection. A cost-neutral propensity branch `q_0(k)=b/k` and its convex-mixture expected-spend drift bound are integrated into the monitor and unit-tested. A 30-seed pilot verified spend stability but did not improve harm detection over uniform querying. A four-value CUDA tolerance grid passed all expected-spend checks but was exploratory (10 paired seeds); see `outputs/cost_tolerance_grid_exploratory/reports/tolerance_grid.md`. In a fresh 60-seed matched-bound hard-budget study (common `q_min=0.10`, 20% target spend), cost-stabilized allocation detected 12/60 boundary-harm streams versus 8/60 for uniform (paired difference `+0.067`, 95% seed-bootstrap CI `[0.000, 0.150]`), with similar RMSE and about 44 more labels. The interval touches zero and is unadjusted across methods; this is not an established benefit. All five methods had 0/60 false escalations in stationary and benign-nuisance streams; exact 95% upper bound is 0.060. See `outputs/hard_budget_matched_qmin_010_replication_60/reports/matched_bound_replication_analysis.md`. The next step is power analysis and a fresh evaluation block only if the candidate benefit remains plausible; do not claim a general optimum. A theoretically justified eventwise e-process bound remains a separate possibility, but would require conditioning on current covariates, not just past history.

   A project-derived exact one-step allocation now maximizes the live fixed-mixture e-value's conditional expected log increment minus a dual label price. The objective is concave in `q` when factors are positive; the derivative and current mixture posterior stake are tested. This is a model-based myopic optimization, not a long-horizon power guarantee, and adaptive e-evidence allocation is already adjacent to e-PS (2026) and active sequential testing. In a locked 60-seed CUDA comparison at a common hard dollar cap, the new rule reduced eventwise prediction-powered mean-error RMSE relative to uniform from 0.0489 to 0.0160 (paired difference −0.0329, 95% seed-bootstrap CI [−0.0425, −0.0232]). But it detected 0/60 boundary-harm streams versus 2/60 for uniform; both harm onsets were reached on all streams, and the paired detection difference was −0.033 (95% bootstrap interval [−0.083, 0.000], exact two-sided McNemar p=0.50). It also spent $9.10 more on average and processed 28.1 percentage points fewer events. No method raised a false escalation in either 60-stream population-null control, and no hard-cap violation occurred. This is evidence of better estimation, not better decision-harm detection; the estimator gain does not support advancing this acquisition rule as the primary method. The independent-seed benchmark, paired metrics, figure and manifest are in `outputs/exact_mixture_growth_matched_60/`; the locked assumptions and reproduction command are in `docs/exact_mixture_growth_protocol.md`.

   An additional exploratory policy, `evidence_growth_proxy`, solves a constrained KKT allocation for the pre-monitor model's stake-weighted expected log-wealth increment. The per-event objective is concave when betting factors are positive; `q(x)` is clipped to stated propensity bounds and a dual multiplier matches expected validation label spend. This is a global optimum only for that model-based surrogate, not the exact log-mixture wealth, realized alternative, or detection power. A fresh 30-seed CUDA block found that the raw rule's mean propensity rose to 13.9% under benign nuisance shift versus its 10% validation rate, with no boundary-harm detections (0/30 versus 1/30 for uniform PPI). A uniform-mixture rate-stabilized variant with a predeclared 0.01 rate-drift tolerance held mean propensity near 10%, but also detected 0/30 boundary-harm streams versus 3/30 for uniform (paired rate difference -0.10; 95% paired bootstrap interval [-0.233, 0.000]). The interval is unadjusted and the event count is small; the present evidence does not justify using this allocation as a primary method. See `outputs/evidence_growth_stabilized_replication_30/reports/evidence_growth_paired_comparison.md`. This objective does not change the validity theorem, which still depends on predictable propensities and the global PPI bound.

   A separate prequential proxy-Hedge development benchmark tested zero, ridge, random-forest, logistic risk-model, validation-selected, and online convex-aggregation proxies on common 20% query paths (100 seeds per scenario, CUDA generation). The Hedge weights update by inverse-propensity squared loss only after queried outcomes; predictable convex proxy mixing preserves the PPI correction identity and support bound. Hedge improved macro detection over ridge by 4.25 points (seed-cluster 95% interval +2.25 to +6.50), but the static logistic risk proxy outperformed Hedge by 3.50 points (post-hoc interval +1.00 to +6.25) and had lower estimation RMSE. The adaptive proxy is therefore a valid comparator, not a lead algorithm. No method detected residual-variance harm, and 0/100 null alarms do not establish calibration. See `docs/prequential_proxy_hedge_protocol.md` and `outputs/prequential_proxy_hedge_development/`.
   The registered logistic decision-risk proxy stress grid used 12 paired seeds per cell across 180 severity, cost, training-share, and query-rate cells. Among the 1,179 material-harm streams per method, it reduced PPI estimator RMSE relative to ridge by 0.213 (95% seed-cluster interval [−0.230, −0.197]) and validation-best by 0.042 ([−0.060, −0.026]), but macro detection differences were unresolved against ridge (+0.21 points, [−2.23, +2.36]), validation-best (−0.10, [−0.56, +0.25]), and Hedge (−0.55, [−2.10, +0.76]). Balanced-cost classifier/boundary cells where the policies coincide are structural zero-harm cases and were excluded from conditional power; no pre-onset escalation occurred, but there was no nondegenerate null calibration. Treat logistic risk prediction as a strong variance-reduction baseline rather than a monitoring contribution. The next test should compare established bounded-mean and variance-sensitive anytime monitors with this fixed proxy on matched paths and nondegenerate nulls; see `docs/risk_proxy_stress_grid_results.md`.

4. **Candidate promotion.** Construct an independent or cross-fitted evaluation protocol for repeated shadow candidates, with simultaneous or online error control, explicit deployment costs, and rollback. Distinguish improvement against a fixed reference from counterfactual policy-value estimation.

   A finite candidate-grid benchmark compares validation-rank spending, equal spending, and validation-only power weights for sequential and empirical-Bernstein gates. Each candidate weight is fixed before independent shadow feedback; the finite-family sum is checked against the familywise level. In 30 CUDA seeds, rank-spending empirical Bernstein had higher promotion rates than equal spending under boundary-local harm (30/30 versus 25/30) and recurring regimes (30/30 versus 22/30), with paired expected-net-gain differences of 42.5 (95% CI 0.2–84.9) and 65.3 (14.5–116.1). Validation-power weights did not improve over rank spending, and pilot-weighted multiple testing is established prior work. Exact-copy controls had 0/180 certificates under each allocation/gate combination; this is a diagnostic, not the proof of familywise validity. See `outputs/promotion_grid_alpha_final/` and `docs/methods_and_assumptions.md`.

   The timing comparison is implemented in `outputs/promotion_timing_v6/`. Across 1,000 independent CUDA streams per schedule, both sequential processes had 32/1,000 promotions under the no-benefit null (3.2%, Wilson 95% CI 2.3–4.5%). In the late-onset persistent regime, the three-start mixture promoted 998/1,000 versus 688/1,000 for the full-start process and 520/1,000 for empirical Bernstein; mean oracle post-promotion net gains were 212.6 (95% t CI 211.5–213.8), 120.5 (115.4–125.7), and 74.9 (70.4–79.3), respectively. In the benefit-reversal scenario, full-start and three-start processes promoted 997/1,000 and 996/1,000 candidates after an early positive phase had already triggered, and mean oracle future net gains were −30.1 (−30.8 to −29.4) and −35.2 (−36.0 to −34.4). Both fixed-horizon gates promoted 1/1,000 and avoided most of that reversal, at the cost of waiting until the fixed horizon. These are controlled synthetic outcomes, not evidence that fixed-horizon testing is generally preferable. The e-process null is eventwise; rejecting it during a positive phase does not certify that future benefit persists. The multi-start and fixed-horizon procedures are established baselines, so no novelty claim follows.

   A two-phase intersection-union gate is now implemented in `outputs/persistence_promotion_grid/`. It uses disjoint fixed discovery and persistence windows and tests the union null that at least one phase has nonpositive eventwise conditional gain. Each phase e-process uses level 0.05; conjunctive rejection controls the union null at 0.05 without an alpha split. In 1,000 CUDA streams, the 0.50 discovery split reduced benefit-reversal promotions to 6/1,000 (0.6%, 95% Wilson interval 0.3–1.3%) from 997/1,000 for the full-start e-process. Mean oracle future net gain was still negative (−0.372 [−0.669, −0.075]), while never promoting yielded zero. For persistent benefit, the same split promoted 974/1,000 and returned mean expected net gain 155.95 [153.89, 158.02], below the full-start process's 255.73 [254.31, 257.15]. The 0.75 split promoted only 42/1,000 in the late-onset scenario, versus 998/1,000 for the multi-start process. The standard intersection-union construction is not novel; the current rule did not dominate the utility frontier.

   The next falsifiable target is now implemented as a predeclared cost-aware robustness grid in `outputs/promotion_robustness_grid_corrected/`: six evaluator-only benefit schedules (no benefit, persistent benefit, and four reversal schedules at early/short, early/long, middle/long, and late/short), four one-time switching charges (0, 10, 30, 60), and paired comparisons of full-start, three-start, fixed-horizon, persistence-certified, and never-promote rules, with 1,000 CUDA streams per schedule. No-benefit full-start promotion fell from 34/1,000 at zero cost to 2/1,000 at cost 60. Under the early long reversal at zero cost, full-start promoted 1,000/1,000 and averaged −18.01 future net value (95% t CI −18.70 to −17.31), while the persistence splits promoted only 7–8/1,000. Yet under the middle long reversal, the 0.25 split promoted all streams and had 22.8% nonpositive post-promotion net outcomes; a later reversal can arrive after confirmation. At charge 30 under persistent benefit, the 0.50 persistence split promoted 61.4% with mean net value 72.85, versus 98.8% and 193.19 for full-start. The late short reversal also shows that a 0.75 discovery split can miss a beneficial opportunity. Each charge level reuses the same generated potential outcomes and includes the charge in the tested gain and post-promotion net utility. The complete grid is reported without selecting a discovery fraction on evaluation data; intervals are descriptive and not multiplicity-adjusted. These results show a strong safety/opportunity-cost trade-off and no uniformly best split or new-algorithm claim. Repeated-candidate alpha allocation and matched label budgets remain open requirements. A future-value rule may only be pursued if validation-only selection predicts a reproducible held-out net-utility gain.

   A predictable mixture of four existing promotion e-process increments was evaluated as a fresh, non-novel baseline. With prior-wealth softmax weights and a fixed 10% uniform floor, the mixture is itself a nonnegative supermartingale when every component has the same null, filtration, support bound and predictable update. In a separate 2,000-stream CUDA run, aggregate-zero familywise crossing was at most 1.60% in any grid cell (strict-negative maximum 0.05%). At 13 candidates and the tight bound, positive-alternative detection was 2.35% versus 7.10% for the fixed mixture at a 20% cap, and 23.95% versus 30.75% at a 40% cap. Paired differences were −4.75 and −6.80 percentage points (95% paired intervals [−5.68, −3.82] and [−7.92, −5.68]); at 10% the methods were effectively tied at zero power. This simple adaptation did not improve on the fixed process and is retained as a comparator, not a proposed contribution. See `docs/online_process_mixture_protocol.md` and `outputs/online_process_mixture_2000/`.

## Decision-local predictable betting bounds

**Construction:** Normalize each candidate's direct-IPW or prediction-powered increment by its exact eventwise support bound, computed from `X_t`, its frozen policy action, the two possible outcome costs, the predictable query propensity, the prequential proxy, and amortized switching cost. This decision-aware scale can be smaller when a candidate's action contrast is locally small.

**Known argument:** Under a pointwise null conditional on both prior history and current covariates, randomized querying with a known positive propensity yields an unbiased paired-gain estimate conditional on those covariates. A predictable positive bound and fixed stake in `[0,1]` produce a nonnegative one-step supermartingale factor. Fixed mixtures and validation-frozen familywise alpha allocations preserve the standard anytime and finite-family control. This is standard betting/e-process reasoning, not a novel proof.

**Initial evidence:** A CUDA grid used 2,000 independent streams per cell, one fixed candidate, 600-event horizons, three label caps, three heterogeneous cost ratios, and one nondegenerate conditional null plus two pointwise alternatives. Under the pointwise null, maximum observed crossing was 44/2,000 (2.2%) for local bounds and 46/2,000 (2.3%) for global bounds, below 5%; this supports implementation sanity but is not the proof. At cost ratio 5 and a 30% cap, the `p=0.56` pointwise alternative crossed 415/2,000 times locally versus 280/2,000 globally, a matched difference of +6.75 points (95% t interval +5.16 to +8.34). At 20% cap the difference was +3.45 points (+2.00 to +4.90). The methods were identical when cost ratio was 1. All 27 intervals are unadjusted, so this is an exploratory signal, not confirmed superiority. Crucially, rejecting the pointwise null is not evidence of positive aggregate mean net gain. When embedded in the 13-candidate hard-budget promotion grid, the local-bound mode had zero crossings in all 15,120 stream-policy rows, despite its mean eventwise bound being 6.9% of the global scale. See `outputs/decision_local_bound_calibration/`, `outputs/budgeted_promotion_grid_local_bound/`, and the methods report.

**Open issue:** The global mode tests a history-conditional average-gain null; decision-local scaling only tests the pointwise conditional null and cannot certify aggregate benefit when subgroup gains have mixed signs. Its zero-crossing result in the candidate grid makes it unsuitable as the current promotion gate. If retained for localized diagnosis, compare it with the predictable plug-in empirical-Bernstein confidence sequences of [Waudby-Smith and Ramdas](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/86/1/1/7043257) and direct paired-loss monitoring at matched error and label budgets. The next mathematical route should preserve the aggregate estimand while exploiting predictable conditional variance or stratified evidence with an explicit mixture/weighting argument. A mixed-sign average-null simulation now verifies the estimand mismatch empirically: when equally likely strata have conditional gains +0.12 and -0.12, aggregate mean gain is exactly zero, yet decision-local crossings ranged from 2.45% to 5.75% across cost ratios 2 and 5 and query caps 10%, 20%, and 30% (2,000 streams per cell). The global-bound crossing rate ranged from 1.2% to 2.05%, below the nominal 5% in this finite calibration. This supports the distinction but is not a replacement for the conditional-expectation proof. See `outputs/mixed_mean_null_local_bound/`. Exact-copy controls remain only a code-path diagnostic.

## Repeated-candidate hard-budget result

A 60-seed CUDA experiment adds 13 repeated candidates under shared 10% and 20% pathwise label caps, comparing direct IPW and prediction-powered e-processes under uniform, variance, and decision-value querying. All seven scenario families had no certificates at the 10% cap. At 20%, prediction-powered monitoring still had none; direct IPW with rank spending promoted 18.3–21.7% of boundary-local streams and 23.3–28.3% of recurring-regime streams, with no certificates in the exact-copy family or other controls. Rank allocation improved paired expected net gain over equal allocation in those alternatives, while query-policy contrasts were mixed. This isolates a useful engineering result—bound choice can dominate power at scarce labels—but does not establish a better query policy or a new method. The intervals are descriptive and the 77 contrast families are unadjusted. The next experiment should be a locked confirmation of a specific boundary/recurring alternative against a stronger variance-adaptive confidence sequence or empirically sharpened valid bound, with explicit power and familywise-error targets. It should retain a fresh exact-copy global-null family and use independent seeds. Do not infer false-promotion control from the zero-event diagnostic alone. See `outputs/budgeted_promotion_grid_ipw/` and `docs/methods_and_assumptions.md`.

## Predictable stake calibration

The globally bounded aggregate-mean null admits predictable variance- and mean-adaptive stakes without dividing each observation by a current-covariate-specific bound. `PredictableVarianceAdaptiveEProcess` uses a smoothed plug-in Kelly stake; its validity follows from the supermartingale condition for any predictable stake in `[0,1]`. A matched CUDA grid includes fixed mixture at alpha and alpha/2, plug-in Kelly, published PM-EB, full-alpha one-sided positive betting, and the canonical half-weighted hedged positive branch. In the fresh 36-cell run (2,000 streams per cell, seed `24102026`), every rule stayed below 5% crossings in each exact zero-mean mixed-sign null cell and below 1% in each strict negative-mean cell. Under broad-positive alternatives, pooled crossing rates were 11.31% fixed mixture, 6.51% fixed mixture at half alpha, 11.43% Kelly, 5.10% PM-EB, 12.14% full-alpha positive betting, and 6.51% half-weighted hedge. For high-cost-only positives, they were 5.95%, 3.33%, 6.33%, 2.33%, 7.22%, and 3.49%. Full-alpha positive betting's paired high-cost-only advantage over fixed mixture was +1.27 percentage points (unadjusted 95% interval +0.95 to +1.59); its broad-positive interval included zero. The half-weighted hedge was indistinguishable from the fixed mixture at the matched alpha/2 threshold on both alternatives. This replication is exploratory, not preregistered, and crossing is only a proxy for promotion quality. The next experiment should freeze a protocol and a fresh seed family, then integrate the best supported baselines into the repeated-candidate budgeted benchmark and compare false promotion, detection delay, label use, and oracle net decision value. No new betting algorithm or novelty claim is supported yet. Results are in `outputs/predictable_betting_comparison/` and `outputs/hedged_betting_comparison_final_24102026/`.

### Repeated-candidate monitoring comparison

A fresh, frozen comparison is implemented in `outputs/promotion_monitor_comparison_60/` and specified in `docs/promotion_monitor_comparison_protocol.md`. It uses 60 seeds (`25102026` onward), seven scenarios, 13 validation-frozen candidates, direct IPW, uniform queries, rank-based candidate alpha spending, global support bounds, and 10%/20% hard label caps. Six established monitors are compared on common seed-specific streams: fixed mixture at alpha and alpha/2, predictable Kelly, PM-EB, full-alpha positive betting, and the half-weighted hedged positive branch at alpha/2. CUDA synthetic generation ran on an RTX 4060 Laptop GPU; the saved manifest records the runtime and checksums. There are 5,040 seed-level rows.

At the 20% cap, fixed mixture promoted 12/60 boundary-local-harm streams (20.0%, Wilson 95% CI 11.8–31.8%) and returned mean evaluator-known future net gain 56.10 (95% t CI 26.48–85.73). Full-alpha positive betting promoted 8/60 (13.3%, 6.9–24.2%) with mean net gain 39.43 (12.98–65.88); its paired net-gain difference from fixed mixture was −16.67 (paired bootstrap 95% CI −34.36 to −3.75). Under recurring regimes, fixed mixture promoted 21/60 (35.0%) and positive betting 14/60 (23.3%); under harmful adaptation, the corresponding counts were 16/60 (26.7%) and 8/60 (13.3%). PM-EB made no promotions in these scenarios at either tested budget, indicating low power in this short, bounded, label-limited setup. Every monitor made zero promotions in the exact-copy family and in benign nuisance shift; the exact-copy family has identically zero paired outcomes, so its 0/60 is a degenerate implementation check, not a meaningful empirical calibration test. Even a nondegenerate 0/60 rate has a Wilson upper limit near 6%, so this block is too small to demonstrate 5% calibration.

These comparisons are descriptive, with unadjusted intervals across scenarios and methods. They suggest a power-threshold tradeoff among established gates; they do not establish a new method or select a winner. The immediate next step is to validate event-level behavior against nondegenerate aggregate-null streams, characterize power as a function of support-bound tightness and number of labels, and then run a larger, separately frozen calibration/power study before designing a new process. The report includes the full cell table and paired contrasts; outputs are hashed in `outputs/promotion_monitor_comparison_60/reports/manifest.json`.

### Nondegenerate repeated-candidate null calibration

The frozen CUDA grid in `docs/repeated_candidate_null_calibration_protocol.md` tests a genuinely stochastic aggregate-mean null across 1, 5, and 13 distinct candidates, 10%/20%/40% hard label caps, and valid support scales 1×/1.5×/2×, using 2,000 streams per cell. On the zero-mean DGP, all candidate policies have nonzero paired-cost variance but exactly zero expected aggregate gain. The familywise false-promotion rate was at most 1.75% across the entire grid (the maximum cell was predictable Kelly, 1 candidate, 40% cap, 1× bound: 35/2,000; Wilson 95% upper limit 2.42%). Under the strict negative control, the maximum was 1/2,000 (0.05%; upper limit 0.28%). This is finite-sample calibration evidence for this DGP, not a proof; the proof remains the supermartingale and union-bound argument.

Power was strongly constrained by label budget and bound scale. For 13 candidates and a tight valid bound, positive-gain detection at 10%, 20%, and 40% caps was 0.45%, 7.15%, and 29.45% for fixed mixture; 0%, 3.55%, and 30.45% for predictable Kelly; and 0%, 0%, and 0.05% for PM-EB. At the 40% cap, doubling the support bound reduced fixed-mixture power from 29.45% to 4.8%. Positive betting and its half-weighted branch fell between the fixed mixture/Kelly and PM-EB in most of these cells. These are pointwise grid results; paired intervals and the full 972,000-row stream table are in `outputs/repeated_candidate_null_calibration_2000/`. They show why a new gate must retain the strict global support guarantee while improving evidence efficiency under a scarce-label cap. No winner or novelty claim follows.
