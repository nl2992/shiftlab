# Action-matched adaptive acquisition versus fixed calendars

**Registered:** 2026-10-28 before running the comparison. **Question:** does label-contingent query timing improve on every deterministic query calendar when all policies have the same single terminal deployment decision?

## Frozen contract

Use `T=6`, pathwise query cap `B=3`, delay `d=1`, causal-null false-promotion level `alpha=1/10`, query fee `c=1/100`, switch fee `k=1/10`, and the recurrent HMM with symmetric transition probabilities `1/4`, emissions `(1/20,19/20)`, and prior `1/2`. The HMM controls expected economic loss; the null's endpoint conditional response controls probability of any promotion. Queries are initiated before the horizon's terminal decision, consume budget at request time, and release after one round. Every policy is prohibited from changing the deployed action before round `T`; at round `T` it may either retain action 0 or promote to action 1. Thus promotion exposure, action timing, and switching opportunities are identical.

The adaptive-acquisition learner may choose whether to query in each eligible round as a function of its public action/query history and all labels released by that time. Its total queries may never exceed `B`. Enumerate all deterministic calendars of at most `B` eligible query rounds; a calendar queries at exactly its scheduled rounds, independent of outcomes. On each calendar, optimize the same terminal action rule. The null adversary observes queries and uses the same causal conditional endpoint class as the unrestricted game. Compare the best fixed-calendar terminal policy with adaptive acquisition plus the same terminal action class. Apply the existing safe root activation calibration against a continuation policy that makes no promotion while honoring that class's query schedule.

## Interpretation and stop rule

A positive gap isolates value from label-contingent acquisition within this registered terminal-decision class, because action timing and rollback are unavailable to both sides. It is still a finite-cell result, not a scalable method or novelty claim; the exact finite oracle is not by itself a publishable algorithm. A zero or negative gap rejects this proposed acquisition advantage in the cell. Do not infer post-reversal benefit from the causal-null promotion guarantee. Record numerical optimization status, independently evaluate recovered policies with exact forward cost and null-risk recursions, and preserve all calendar results and source hashes.

Run `python scripts/compare_switching_action_matched_adaptive_acquisition.py` with the repository source path configured. Save results under `outputs/switching_action_matched_adaptive_acquisition_v1/results.json`.

## Result and composition check

The adaptive LP's recovered rule queries at event 3 with probability `4/5`, continues at event 4 only after label 3 is positive, continues at event 5 only after labels 3 and 4 are positive, and promotes at event 6 only after `111`. Its exact expected cost is `1179063/400000`, its expected query count is `1481/1000`, and its exact causal-null risk is `1/10`. The best of 26 mandatory deterministic calendars is `(3,4,5)`, at cost `1185139/400000`; its recovered terminal test promotes with probability `4/5` on `111`, also with exact risk `1/10`. All 26 fixed-calendar LPs have independently checked rational dual lower bounds. The minimum such lower bound is about `2.962847499999693`, so the explicit adaptive policy's exact feasible cost `2.9476575` proves a certified gap of at least `0.015189999999693` from the entire deterministic-calendar terminal-action class. The adaptive LP optimum remains numerical; this does not certify the adaptive optimum.

An explicit positive-prefix futility policy reproduces the adaptive LP recovered policy's full expected metric tuple and null risk under independent exact forward recursion. A matched randomized-calendar baseline chooses no queries with probability `1/5` or the full `(3,4,5)` cohort with probability `4/5`, promoting on `111` in the latter branch. It has exact expected cost `1182739/400000`, expected queries `12/5`, and null risk `1/10`. The adaptive futility rule saves `919/1000` expected queries and `919/100000` cost against this lottery while preserving the same terminal action on every coupled potential-label tape. This decomposes entry randomization from outcome-contingent stopping in one cell, but does not optimize over every randomized calendar/test composition.

The reduction behind the policy is elementary: couple a randomized all-positive terminal test to the same fixed cohort and stop buying labels after the first zero. On every potential label tape the terminal action is unchanged. Under the endpoint causal null, a promotion requires `m` successive positive queried labels, so its worst-case probability is at most `lambda 2^{-m}` when the entry lottery has mass `lambda`; here `(m,lambda)=(3,4/5)`, giving `1/10`. Under any alternative label law, expected query count becomes `lambda sum_{j=0}^{m-1} P(Y_1=...=Y_j=1)`, with the empty-prefix probability equal to one. This is an exact cost-saving coupling for the all-positive test, not a new sequential-testing principle. Early futility stopping and cost-aware group-sequential testing have long prior art, including [Brown and Rutemiller (1971)](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.19.4.970), [Gould and Pecore (1982)](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/69.1.75), and delayed-response group-sequential testing by [Hampson and Jennison (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2012.01030.x). The remaining possible distinction is the joint switching-utility, hidden-regime, hard-budget and causal-null objective; no novelty claim follows without a source-level equivalence audit against those settings and controlled sequential testing. Historical promotion-risk control remains distinct from current-regime evidence and future utility after a reversal.
