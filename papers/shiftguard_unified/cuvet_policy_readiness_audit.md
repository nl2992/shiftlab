# CUVET-Policy source and task-readiness audit

**Audit date:** 2026-10-26
**Status:** complete train split streamed once for aggregate-only auditing; test split remains unread; no policy values computed and no final protocol frozen.
**Machine constraint:** the dataset files total 13,713,548,892 bytes (about 12.8 GiB); the current C: volume has about 343 MiB free.

## Source and observed access

The [Hugging Face dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/anonadata/CUVET-policy) reports CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, 86.7 million rows, five continuous context features, randomized treatment multipliers `{0.8, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1, 1.2}`, `value` and `spend` outcomes, a random train/test split, and a validation flag within train. The two public CSV objects are 10,317,675,360 bytes (train) and 3,395,873,532 bytes (test). Their source object hashes and the Hugging Face dataset revision are recorded in [`cuvet_policy_source_audit.json`](../../data/reports/cuvet_policy_source_audit.json).

The authors' [NeurIPS 2025 Workshop on Uncovering Causality in Science paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=ue4YbN3wgh) describes the source as a two-week randomized A/B test from an online advertising platform. Each record represents an independent bidding opportunity with five-dimensional context, one of five randomized bid multipliers, value, and cost. Its policy-learning objective is to maximize expected value while constraining expected cost to be no greater than the expected cost of the reference treatment. The data card says value and spend were scaled and perturbed for confidentiality; they must not be described as currency or realized profit.

The pinned 10,317,675,360-byte training CSV was streamed successfully in aggregate-only mode (65,031,420 parsed rows, zero malformed rows, zero missing cells). Empirical arm shares were 8.4836% for 0.8, 9.2179% for 0.9, 60.2535% for reference 1.0, 10.6928% for 1.1, and 11.3521% for 1.2. Max absolute standardized mean differences across the five released features versus reference ranged from 0.0068 to 0.0133. The `is_val` flag was populated in every treatment arm at approximately one-third of rows. A follow-up tabulation across ten 10-million-wide source-row-ID bands found each arm's share within 0.057 percentage points of its full-train share (maximum deviation, reference arm); this weakens concern about large assignment-share changes across those ID bands, but the ID is anonymized and is not a verified time field. The paper and data card state that treatments were randomized but publish no numerical assignment probabilities or detailed randomization mechanism. Thus full-train empirical shares and feature-balance summaries remain descriptive estimates, **not documented design propensities or proof of conditional randomization**. No raw rows were written to disk, and the 3.40 GB official test file was queried only for metadata, not read. The dataset had not otherwise appeared in the ShiftLab repository before this audit (`rg` search returned no matches); published CUVET results have used the same benchmark, so it is not novel to the research community.

The same full-train pass now includes aggregate outcomes by treatment and `is_val` partition. The `is_val=0` per-row mean value for multipliers `(0.8,0.9,1.0,1.1,1.2)` is `(2.6016e-4, 2.4326e-4, 3.8072e-5, 2.1760e-4, 2.0611e-4)`; mean spend is `(0.05734,0.06009,0.01038,0.06466,0.06717)`. Log-log OLS slopes over these five arm means give `γ=-0.7481` for value and `η=0.2072` for spend. The independently held-out `is_val=1` partition is similar (`γ=-0.7743`, `η=0.2075`). The paper resolves the relevant aggregation: Section C.1 defines a bucket outcome as the conditional mean multiplied by the bucket's population mass, so per-row arm means (then scaled by a common bucket mass) are the relevant arm comparisons. Raw outcome sums by treatment instead inherit the strongly unequal treatment counts and are not a valid substitute; their positive value slope is count-driven. Thus the released means violate CUVET's assumed increasing value curve and `γ<η` ordering at the root. This is a descriptive source-data/model compatibility finding, not a causal conclusion and not evidence that assignment was non-random. The exploratory tree fitter correctly rejected all depths rather than altering or clipping the response elasticity. See `cuvet_policy_sample_development.json` and the exact aggregate fields in `cuvet_policy_source_audit.json`.

A prespecified train-only feature-predictability diagnostic sampled 845,867 rows from 128 distributed byte ranges (at most 128 MiB transferred), trained with the RTX 4060, and held out 132,168 sampled rows in 20 disjoint byte-range blocks for diagnostic scoring. A prevalence-only classifier achieved held-out diagnostic log loss 1.21928 and accuracy 60.341%; linear multinomial regression had log loss 1.21928 (paired gain -0.000001, descriptive range-block 95% interval [-0.000062, 0.000061]); the nonlinear MLP had log loss 1.21947 (gain -0.000191, interval [-0.000326, -0.000057]). All three predicted the majority reference arm, with 20% balanced accuracy. These models found no held-out predictive benefit from the five released features; the MLP performed slightly worse than prevalence-only. This is evidence against strong predictable dependence detectable by these model classes in this sampled diagnostic, **not** proof of random assignment, a formal randomization test, or evidence against hidden confounding. The intervals treat byte ranges as independent clusters and are descriptive only. The official dataset test file was not accessed. Full metrics and provenance are in [`cuvet_assignment_predictability.json`](../../data/reports/cuvet_assignment_predictability.json), produced by [`audit_cuvet_assignment_predictability.py`](../../scripts/audit_cuvet_assignment_predictability.py).

## Suitability for ShiftLab

This remains a candidate for **real, fixed-horizon, cost-constrained intervention evaluation**, but the full-train response diagnostic raises a material compatibility issue for the published CUVET model. All five arms are present, declared cells are populated, and marginal feature means are balanced to within 0.014 SD of the reference. These facts support basic data adequacy but do not establish assignment probabilities or causal identification. The source's `value` and `spend` columns support an observed outcome comparison under a justified randomized-design assumption; however, their per-row means do not follow the increasing power-law response assumed by CUVET, and the paper's bucket definition confirms that treatment-count-weighted sums are not the remedy. Do not fit the paper's CUVET optimizer to this release. Flexible outcome-model and supported-arm policy methods remain possible, subject to assignment assumptions and cost-feasibility analysis.

It does **not** support sequential monitoring or delayed-feedback claims: although the experiment ran for two weeks, the released files contain no event timestamp, feature-availability timestamp, or label-release time. The row split is random rather than chronological. Exact treatment assignment probabilities are not included in the card's schema. The source says assignment is random, and the train-only sample is consistent with randomized assignment, but the mechanism and probabilities must be verified before using inverse-propensity or sequential guarantees. Do not treat the sample shares as known propensities.

## Published comparator and result audit

The primary CUVET paper is itself a prior evaluation of this same benchmark and already reports use of its official test split. Its comparator set is explicit: EconML causal forests with heterogeneity-score (CF-Het) and MSE (CF-MSE) splitting, plus clipped and unclipped CUVET. Each learned partition is paired with three budget solvers: continuous mCUV equalization, deterministic ILP, and stochastic LP. The causal-forest outcome is a value-cost proxy because EconML accepts one outcome. Its specification is inconsistent: Appendix H.4 gives `y = v − (γ/η)c`, while the synthetic-example appendix gives `y = v − (η/γ)c`. These are generally different coefficients; the paper does not establish which was used for its real-data table. This proxy construction and all tree depth/model-selection details must be matched or deviations named. The CUVET tree estimates log-linear value and cost elasticities for candidate splits; its appendix specifies max depths `{1,2,3}`, leaf/sample split thresholds as functions of sample size and depth, and clipping to the observed treatment interval. The paper states that it used the validation split to select models on CUVET-Policy.

The paper reports relative value/spend uplifts on the test split, with ten bootstraps and intervals `1.96 × σ`. In its Table 2, clipped/unclipped CUVET under mCUV equalization have value uplifts `0.0103 ± 0.0033` / `0.0098 ± 0.0036` and spend uplifts `0.0019 ± 0.0013` / `0.0010 ± 0.0010`. Under ILP, these are value `0.0149 ± 0.0100` / `0.0118 ± 0.0060` and spend `0.0045 ± 0.0038` / `−0.0013 ± 0.0021`. Under LP, they are value `0.0094 ± 0.0096` / `0.0155 ± 0.0109` and spend `0.0035 ± 0.0043` / `0.0023 ± 0.0029`. CF-Het and CF-MSE are also reported for all three solvers. The source prose describes mCUV as a roughly 1% value gain with a slight budget violation, while a parenthetical says `Δc_B = 0`; the table instead gives positive spend uplifts for both CUVET variants. Use the tabulated values and explicitly record this prose/table inconsistency. A positive value uplift with positive spend uplift does not establish compliance with a hard spend cap. Ten bootstrap replicates are also too few to substitute for a well-calibrated confirmatory interval.

These are contextual published results, not independent ShiftLab evidence. Since the source authors already evaluated the official test split, ShiftLab may use it as a sealed split relative to our own model selection, but must not call it a previously unused or community-unseen holdout. A ShiftLab reproduction should compare the paper's full CF/CUVET-by-solver matrix, apply an explicitly frozen paired uncertainty procedure, and state whether the result reproduces the source estimand. The paper provides useful algorithm details, but Algorithm 1 leaves its candidate-split generator abstract and the causal-forest proxy coefficient conflicts between Appendix H.4 and Appendix F. A targeted paper-link and repository search surfaced the dataset and MT-LIFT repository links but no CUVET algorithm repository; this does not prove that no implementation exists. The published CF real-data baseline therefore cannot be reconstructed unambiguously from the paper alone. Implementation fidelity remains open until source code/configuration is recovered or a clearly labeled adaptation is independently checked.

The bucket-level mCUV optimizer is implemented in [`src/shiftlab/dive/cuvet.py`](../../src/shiftlab/dive/cuvet.py) and tested against the paper's Appendix F numerical example. Weighting the three listed conditional bucket totals by the `Uniform(0,1)` region masses gives a reference budget of `0.13`; the implementation returns multipliers `(1.1911, 1.1984, 0.6388)`, shadow price `0.10074`, and total modeled cost `0.13`, matching the paper's approximate `(1.2, 1.2, 0.63)` and `0.1`. Eight focused tests cover the allocator and an exploratory quantile-tree fitter, including rejection of this dataset's invalid root elasticity. These checks validate the allocation equation and guard against an invalid fit; they do not reproduce the full paper method. Candidate-split generation is an explicit adaptation, and causal-forest baselines and real-data policy evaluation remain unimplemented.

The complete train file was processed remotely in bounded memory in 350 seconds, so local disk shortage does not prevent full train analysis. The test file is about 3.40 GB and remains untouched. A final evaluation must be independently replayable, record the pinned revision and object hashes, keep the official test split untouched until freeze, and write only aggregate metrics and manifests. The dataset's CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 terms require attribution, non-commercial use, and share-alike treatment of adaptations; raw rows should remain out of Git and out of generated figures.

## Gate decision and next test

**The outcome aggregation question is resolved; the CUVET response mismatch remains.** Section C.1 of the [CUVET paper](https://openreview.net/pdf/f6df1c03b6cbd749a33edec2613084e468539328.pdf) defines bucket outcomes as conditional arm means multiplied by a shared bucket population mass. The observed per-row arm means therefore give the relevant root response comparison, and their negative value elasticity is not repaired by raw sums: those sums are dominated by the unequal treatment counts. Stop a faithful CUVET reproduction on this dataset unless source authors document a different outcome encoding or the released benchmark is shown to differ from the paper's data. Separately, the paper/card say treatment was randomized but publish no exact design probabilities. A constant arm-propensity model is only a working assumption. For a flexible supported-arm policy comparison, freeze training-only propensity estimation and sensitivity, and limit causal claims accordingly. The official test may be used only after the task contract and method are resolved and the protocol frozen; the source paper already used that split.

A non-frozen protocol draft now records the proposed split roles, estimand, candidate set, IPW/DR evaluation, propensity sensitivity, and stop rules in [`cuvet_fixed_horizon_policy_protocol_draft.md`](cuvet_fixed_horizon_policy_protocol_draft.md). It is not authorization to inspect the official test; the source design and baseline fidelity checks listed in that draft remain open.

This candidate could close a real downstream allocation gap, but it is one offline task family and does not replace the required temporal-monitoring evaluation. It also does not, by itself, satisfy the full requirement for two independent real-data task families with final evaluation.
