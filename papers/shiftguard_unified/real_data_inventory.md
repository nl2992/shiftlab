## Evidence and exposure limits

- The representative processed Parquet files are readable in this workspace, and their schema includes event, feature-availability, prediction, label-event, label-availability, domain, split, and metadata fields. Those schema fields do not make source publication times verified; the readiness audit marks many as unknown.
- The data-readiness reports classify Gas Sensor, WILDS, Folktables, CRSP, Criteo Attribution, and CriteoPrivateAds as offline, coarse-domain, retrospective, or delayed-feedback tasks rather than verified live deployment replays.
- Existing diagnostics include Gas Sensor group classification, time-series prediction diagnostics, Monash official-horizon baselines, CRSP smoke/replays, and many synthetic studies. The source-specific prior-use audit is incomplete; do not yet designate any inspected real test set as a final untouched holdout.
- CRSP raw and derived data, Criteo raw data, and all other local datasets are ignored by Git in this checkout. This report contains aggregate sizes/counts only; do not add licensed or private observations or credentials to a public commit. Confirm source-specific redistribution terms before copying local data to any remote.
- The latest CRSP panel is not represented in the central data manifest/inventory. Dataset manifest entries are versioned but include duplicate/superseded records; use a single explicit version per task in any future protocol.
- CriteoPrivateAds is represented in the source inventory but not the processed dataset manifest because the evaluation streams the publisher's raw Parquet directly without creating a transformed copy. A full aggregate label/delay scan and the cost-sensitive click-prediction comparison are complete; the two-day block convention and immediate/one-day sensitivities remain declared replay assumptions, not observed publisher release times. ## Hillstrom MineThatData email experiment (2026-10-26)

The official [2008 challenge page](https://blog.minethatdata.com/2008/03/minethatdata-e-mail-analytics-and-data.html) offers a 64,000-customer CSV, states customers were randomly assigned across men's email, women's email, and no-email groups, and describes two-week outcomes for visits, conversions, and spend. One local source file was acquired (3,964,977 bytes; SHA-256 `0e5893329d8b93cefecc571777672028290ab69865718020c78c7284f291aece`); aggregate schema checks found 64,000 rows, 12 expected columns, no missing cells, and assignment counts of 21,307, 21,387, and 21,306. The HTTP endpoint has no published cryptographic checksum; this hash is a local reproducibility identifier. No explicit modern license was found; raw data stays local and ignored by Git.

A fixed-horizon development study evaluates a Women's-email-vs-no-email policy at the publisher's 10,000-customer capacity. Outcomes are observed gross USD spend, conversion, and visit; email cost is an imposed sensitivity grid. The CUDA study compares neural and Ridge T-learners, historical-spend targeting, random allocation, no email, and email-all. Checksummed aggregate metrics are in `outputs/shiftguard_unified/real_data_development/hillstrom_email_policy_v3_20261026/`. At the imposed $0.25/email setting, the neural T-learner minus capacity-matched random allocation estimate was **−$0.02578 per customer** (95% row-level Wald interval **[−$0.16538, $0.11381]**), an inconclusive result. The interval assumes independent customers; email costs are not observed campaign costs.

The evaluation split was inspected in v1, reused for a Ridge/paired-contrast extension in v2, and used again in the policy-arm-only scaling correction in v3. It is exposed, not final confirmation; v1/v2 are superseded exploratory outputs. This is one retrospective fixed-horizon intervention task, not sequential monitoring or delayed feedback. See `hillstrom_email_policy_development_protocol.md` and `real_data_development_results.md`.

## CUVET-Policy public advertising treatment benchmark (2026-10-26)

The public Hugging Face source is pinned at revision `36583ac60379ed83fd375c84d2bf8ce1c73869c1`. Its train/test CSVs total 13,713,548,892 bytes. A complete bounded-memory stream of the 10,317,675,360-byte training file parsed 65,031,420 rows with zero malformed records or missing cells. Empirical treatment shares were 8.48%, 9.22%, 60.25% (reference), 10.69%, and 11.35%; maximum absolute feature-mean SMD versus reference was 0.0133. Arm shares varied by at most 0.057 percentage points across ten anonymized source-row-ID bands. A GPU feature-only diagnostic on 845,867 sampled training rows used a held-out 20-range-block split. Prevalence-only, linear, and MLP held-out diagnostic log loss were 1.21928, 1.21928, and 1.21947 respectively; all predicted only the majority arm. This detected no held-out feature-prediction benefit, but does not prove random assignment. The primary paper and dataset card state random assignment but publish no exact probabilities or detailed design. The empirical shares and balance checks are therefore diagnostics, not known design propensities. No raw rows were persisted; the official test file was not read, and no policy value or test metric was computed. See [`cuvet_policy_readiness_audit.md`](cuvet_policy_readiness_audit.md) and [`cuvet_assignment_predictability.json`](../../data/reports/cuvet_assignment_predictability.json).

The authors describe a two-week randomized online-advertising experiment with five bid-multiplier treatments and observed source-scaled value and spend. Their policy objective maximizes expected value subject to expected spend no greater than the reference policy's spend. The public card declares CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. This is a promising **fixed-horizon cost-constrained policy-allocation** task and a different operational setting from email targeting. It has no released event, feature-availability, or feedback-release timestamps; exact assignment probabilities are not documented in the schema. The official test split has not been read by ShiftLab and should remain sealed from our development until the assignment contract and protocol are frozen. The source paper already reports results on this same test split, so it is not a community-unseen holdout. This dataset cannot validate sequential monitoring or real-world dollar profit. See the [CUVET paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=ue4YbN3wgh) and [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/anonadata/CUVET-policy).

This source improves the real-intervention path but remains a candidate rather than qualifying final confirmation. Full files exceed local free disk; bounded HTTP streaming appears feasible, but has not yet been tested over a complete split. It does not by itself close the two-family independent-real-confirmation gate.
