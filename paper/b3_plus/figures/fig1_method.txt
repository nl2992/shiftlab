Setting. A chronological event stream of length T. A frozen incumbent f0 and a frozen challenger
f1 produce 0/1 losses. The deployed action a_t is chosen before the label Y_t is seen. A label is
obtainable only by paying c_q for it, at most B labels per episode, and a requested label is
released d events later. Each switch of the deployed model costs c_s. The objective is the
realised net cost: deployment loss + c_q N_q + c_s N_s.

Per-event cycle, in three stages. (1) ISSUE: the policy decides a query probability q_t and
freezes a betting fraction lambda_t, using only information released strictly before event t-d.
Only events where f0 and f1 disagree are ever queried, and q_t is floored at p_min so every
eligible disagreement keeps positive propensity. (2) RELEASE: the label of event s = t-d becomes
usable; its importance-weighted paired increment x_s = (l0[s] - l1[s]) / q_s enters a paired
e-process with the lambda_s frozen at its own issue time, giving a time-uniform certificate.
(3) DECIDE: the challenger is promoted when the e-process crosses log(1/alpha); rollback uses the
mirrored test. Decisions take effect from t+1.

The contribution: settled-decision acquisition. The incumbent procedure keeps paying its
budget-paced query rate for the whole episode. But once the challenger has been promoted and the
recent discounted paired advantage still favours it, the deployed action is no longer in question,
and further labels buy only rollback insurance. The policy therefore distinguishes two inert
states in which no label can change the deployment decision: HOPELESS, not yet promoted and the
challenger certified worse, where acquisition falls to a quarter rate; and SETTLED, already
promoted with the challenger still ahead, where acquisition falls to the propensity floor p_min.
Outside those two states pacing is exactly the incumbent's, so the rule can only ever withhold
spend, never exceed it. The evidence process, its propensity-matched bet cap, the promotion
threshold and the rollback test are all unchanged.
