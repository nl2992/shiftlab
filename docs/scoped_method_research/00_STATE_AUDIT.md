## 3. Historical evidence and result status

### Failure Atlas v2, November 3

`docs/failure_atlas_v2_atlas.md`, `docs/failure_atlas_v2_report.md`, and `docs/failure_atlas_v2_validation.md` document discovery on 20 synthetic regimes and nine previously exposed real replay streams, then a registered run on 13 synthetic regimes (100 seeds) and those same nine streams. The common evaluation contract is generally `T=2000, B=200, c_q=.05, c_s=5, alpha=.05`.

The Atlas diagnosed (M1) low/late promotion by error-controlled methods, (M2) aggressive selectors' frequent promotion on null/worse streams, (M3) no post-promotion rollback after reversal, (M4) dependence sensitivity, and (M5) front-loaded budget exhaustion in one port. Its controlled development ablations attribute late-improvement cost to both inverse-propensity range/pacing and cumulative stop-at-verdict evidence. The same docs say that real episodes did not contain within-stream sign reversals, so the reversal diagnosis is synthetic-only in those data.

RMM (restart-mixture label-clock detector plus continued post-verdict paced acquisition) met its registered synthetic family criteria on the historical validation: family cost improvement over its hosts and a roughly 2.8–3.7-unit comparison against the same-validation-selected best controlled comparator. Limitations recorded in the same report are decisive: adverse transient/reversal/recurrent costs, inflated promotion on Markov persistence (about .23–.24 at rho=.8), no advantage versus the strongest controlled comparator on real streams, and same-sample comparator selection. The standalone LRMD registered safety criterion failed at 6/100 boundary-null promotions; the authors noted this was weak evidence for a nominal .05 rate. The validation's registered primary family was frozen after development had already revealed adverse-regime failures; intervals do not account for best-comparator selection.

**DISCERN correction:** `docs/failure_atlas_v2_report.md` explicitly states that all Atlas v2 DISCERN results (including DISCERN+RMM) predate the PrPl-EB `psi_E` `/4` fidelity fix and overstate its variance penalty by a factor of four. The FLQ README repeats that RMM/LRMD runs predate the fix. Those historical DISCERN comparisons cannot be used as faithful corrected-competitor evidence. The fix is in `certificate_stops.py`; the current code documents the author formula and notes that public author code was later located, but numerical parity with the native implementation is still unverified.

The parallel FLQ/QCE line (`docs/failure_atlas_v2_flq/README.md` and its result report) also found that faster query-conditional/front-loaded evidence gains came with dependence-risk failures. ## 8. Evidence classification rule for continuation

Use four labels in subsequent artifacts:

- **Historical:** protocol-registered results in earlier branches/commits, including negative/failed findings; preserve exact commit/run manifest.
- **Correction replay:** same historical stream/seeds rerun after a code correction; necessary to repair fidelity but not independent confirmation.
- **Development:** runs or data used to choose candidate, settings, subgroup, baseline, or claim; descriptive even if seeds are new.
- **Confirmation:** only outcomes generated after a written freeze that was independent of candidate selection and remains untouched until the frozen analysis; independent real evidence additionally requires genuine source clusters, chronological training/prediction, and valid label-release timing.

Fresh RNG seeds alone do not turn development into confirmation. A nominally new dataset is not independent if its outcomes or related source/time windows were already used to select the method. A report/test manifest proves only the assertions it actually checks.
