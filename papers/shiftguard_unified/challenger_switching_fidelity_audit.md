# Sequential challenger-switching comparator audit

**Audit date:** 2026-10-26
**Source:** Digalakis Jr, Pérignon, Saurin, and Sentenac, *The Challenger: When Do New Data Sources Justify Switching Machine Learning Models?*, arXiv:2512.18390v2, revised 2026-07-16. This is an author preprint, not a peer-reviewed proceedings paper at the version checked. Primary source: [arXiv record and full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18390), [HTML full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18390).

## Published task and method

The paper studies an incumbent trained on an established feature set and a challenger that can use newly available features. Since the incumbent's historical data lack those new features, the challenger is trained on a smaller, gradually growing full-feature sample. At decision epochs, the organization chooses to **switch**, **discard** the challenger, or **continue** collecting data and evaluating it. Challenger model training and validation are repeated at epochs; deployment is irreversible in the stated core setup.

The target is economic challenger value. The authors define a paired per-sample performance gap between challenger and incumbent and explicitly allow a bounded monetary gain function that charges false positives and false negatives differently. In the finite-horizon model, the switch value includes pre-decision data costs, a one-time switch cost, and future per-sample gains net of operating costs. The switching rule is based on that future net value. The paper therefore already combines statistical challenger assessment with an explicit costed deployment decision; ShiftGuard must not present that general transition as new.

The paper analyzes the oracle stopping time under a power-law learning curve. Its meta-regret result gives an `O(T^(2/3) sqrt(log T))` bound relative to a full-foresight oracle when (among other conditions) the challenger class has finite VC dimension, training is ERM, the gain is in `{-1,1}`, a terminal decision occurs on the prescribed `T^(2/3)` time scale up to a logarithmic factor, and the switch/discard decision is sufficiently consistent. Practical procedures include Greedy Sequential Evaluation (GSE), One-Shot Evaluation (OSE), and Look-ahead Sequential Evaluation (LSE/LSEc), which use repeated sample splits, finite-epoch confidence bounds, and/or learning-curve extrapolation.

GSE's paper states that Hoeffding bounds plus a union bound over the finite set of decision epochs give simultaneous coverage for its epochwise bounds under its bounded validation-gain setup. This is a valid finite-epoch construction under the paper's stated conditions, but it should not be described as an anytime-valid e-process or an always-valid guarantee under arbitrary conditional drift. The distinction concerns the guarantee and assumptions, not the fact that the comparator makes sequential decisions.

## Fidelity and inclusion decision

**Classification:** high-priority scope comparator for any claim about cost-aware sequential challenger evaluation or model switching. It is not a valid drop-in comparator for the current common-label Q1 benchmark: ShiftGuard's fixed candidate pair does not have a gradually expanding feature set and learning-curve objective. It is also not directly comparable to the current Q3 EIA refit frontier without reproducing its incumbent/expanded-feature task, sample-arrival process, cost structure, evaluation schedule, and value calculation.

**Quantitative run decision:** do not create a synthetic “head-to-head” by mapping ShiftGuard's monitor onto the paper's learning-curve task or vice versa. That would change both the information contract and estimand. A faithful reproduction would require implementing the published GSE/OSE/LSE or LSEc procedures on their credit-scoring data or a newly registered task with the same gradually arriving-feature contract, and comparing with their own oracle/value calculation. The repository currently has no verified copy of the paper's credit data or source code/experiment environment pinned for a reproduction. Until data and methods are acquired and the protocol frozen, record this comparator as **primary-source audited, not reproduced**.

If a later paper claims a distinct ShiftGuard contribution, the falsifiable comparison should demonstrate at least one of the following under a shared, defensible task: (a) valid conditional decision-harm monitoring where finite-epoch expected-value bounds do not answer the target; (b) calibrated intervention control with delayed/selective labels and an explicit adaptive alpha/error budget; or (c) a resource frontier with query, update, switch, and fallback costs where sequential challenger methods are included. These are research requirements, not current results or novelty claims.

## Reproducibility details to verify before any reproduction

- The paper's algorithm relies on decision epochs, random train/validation splits, a bounded performance-gap estimate, cost parameters, and an assumed/estimated future value schedule.
- The finite-horizon asymptotic stopping analysis uses a power-law learning curve; the empirical algorithms also consider arbitrary data arrival and discounted cost settings.
- GSE's bounds are adjusted across the finite epoch set; its terminal decision has a specified rule. Do not implement only the early stopping branch and call it a full reproduction.
- The authors' empirical evaluation uses chronologically ordered credit data and constructs disjoint temporal blocks/sample paths. It also evaluates realized future value using later observations, which is evaluator-side measurement and must not be made learner-visible.
- A source-faithful study needs the exact versioned feature subsets, chronological partitions, costs, model classes, epoch schedule, randomization, confidence parameter, and oracle implementation. The current paper provides a detailed protocol, but ShiftLab has not independently reconstructed its original release artifacts.

**Disposition:** cost-aware sequential switching is established prior work. Any possible ShiftGuard extension lies in a narrower conditional-risk, delayed-feedback, adaptively composed monitoring/intervention setting and remains unverified. Do not claim novelty or comparative superiority until a complete source-faithful baseline and a task-matched independent evaluation are available.
