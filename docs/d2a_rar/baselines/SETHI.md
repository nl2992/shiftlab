## Problem
Ergodic finite-state (|[m]| = m) time-homogeneous chain (P, μ); data X_0, X_1, …, **observed at every step**. H0: P ∈ 𝒫 vs H1: P ∈ 𝒬, 𝒫 ∩ 𝒬 = ∅,
both compact (composite vs composite). α-correct power-one test: stopping time τ with P_{P,μ}[τ<∞] ≤ α ∀P∈𝒫, ∀μ and P_{Q,μ}[τ<∞] = 1 ∀Q∈𝒬.

## Algorithm 1 (one-sided)
Maintain N_x = #{s: X_{s−1}=x}, N_{x,y} = #{s: (X_{s−1},X_s)=(x,y)}. Q̂_t(x,·) = N_{x,·}/N_x (uniform 1/m if N_x = 0).
β_t = Σ_x log( e (1 + N_x/(m−1)) ) [UNVERIFIED form]; boundary ε_t = log(1/α) + (m−1) β_t [UNVERIFIED form].
L_t = inf_{P∈𝒫} Σ_{x:N_x>0} N_x · KL( Q̂_t(x,·) ‖ P(x,·) ). Stop and reject H0 at the first t with L_t ≥ ε_t.
Remark 4.2: exp(L_t − (m−1)β_t) ≤ a nonnegative supermartingale ⇒ e-process (so an anytime-valid evidence stream is available).
The inner inf is a convex program when 𝒫 is convex (paper, Sec 4.1); their code's solver choice is unverified.
**Surrogate (Prop 4.3):** L̃_t = inf_{P∈𝒫} sup_{g} (E_{Q̂_t⋆}[g] − E_P[g])² / (2‖ψ_{P,g}‖²_∞-type), ψ the Poisson-equation solution (I−P)ψ = g − π_P(g)1;
closed form via Prop D.1 (appendix, **unread**). α-correct but conservative (longer τ).
Two-sided (Thm 4.4): run (𝒫 vs 𝒬) and (𝒬 vs 𝒫) in parallel with levels (α, β).

## Guarantees (carry exactly these)
* α-correct (Thm 4.1) w.r.t. the **natural filtration of X** and 1-step kernels in 𝒫.
* limsup_{α→0} E_Q[τ]/log(1/α) ≤ 1/D^M_inf(Q,𝒫), D^M(Q,P) = Σ_i π_Q(i) KL(Q(i,·)‖P(i,·)); lower bound E_Q[τ] ≥ log(1/α)/D^M_inf − 2C_Q/(min_i π_i) (Thm 3.3).
* Asymptotic (α→0) optimality; **no finite-α power guarantee, no guarantee about loss, risk or harm.** Their own MCMC experiment found no false rejections in 100 runs (boundary conservative).

# Assessment — are exact-gap transitions P^{g_i} (irregular observation times) already covered by Sethi et al.?

**Verdict: NO, not as a stated result.** Only two special cases reduce to their theorem; the general case (and the case where the observation mechanism carries information) is outside what the paper proves. This is a reading-based assessment; I did not read the appendix proofs line by line.

Evidence (quotes <15 words; all from the main text/appendix of the PDF):
1. Data model is consecutive: "We assume sequential access to data: at time t we observe Xt." and the chain is "A time-homogeneous Markov chain" generated by a single kernel P. Alg 1 builds counts only from consecutive pairs (X_{t−1}, X_t).
2. Null/alternative are sets of **1-step** transition matrices in M (Sec 1); the statistic is an inf over 𝒫 of a sum of row-KLs of the empirical 1-step kernel — rows are indexed by state only, not by (state, gap).
3. Filtration: stopping times are taken "with respect to the natural filtration generated by" the chain (appendix; Wald/Moustakides argument). Nothing in the paper lets the observation times depend on, or carry information about, the chain.
4. Searching the extracted text for irregular / missing / skipped / thinned / subsampled observation found no match (only "spectral gap" hits). Their listed future directions (Sec 6) are Fields et al. composite extension, statistical–computational trade-off, countable state spaces, and hidden-Markov observations — not irregular sampling.

What *is* covered:
* **Constant gap g (thinning).** If every gap equals g, Y_i = X_{ig} is a time-homogeneous chain with kernel P^g. Apply Sethi to Y with null class 𝒫^{(g)} = {P^g : P∈𝒫} (compact image under continuous map; may be non-convex ⇒ the inner inf needs a non-convex solver, but Thm 4.1 allows arbitrary compact 𝒫). If P is ergodic (irreducible and aperiodic, hence primitive) then P^g is ergodic. This is the "fixed-gap/thinning" comparator and discards the intermediate information.
* **Exogenous i.i.d. gaps via state augmentation (my construction, not in the paper).** If the gaps g_i are i.i.d. from an unknown law h on a finite set, independent of the chain, then Z_i = (Y_i, g_i) is a finite-state chain with kernel Z_{i−1}→Z_i given by h(g_i) P^{g_i}(Y_{i−1},Y_i); Sethi's Thm 4.1 applies to the class {h·P^{(·)} : P∈𝒫, h∈Δ(G)}. Validity holds, but the nuisance h inflates both the class (inf over h) and the m(|G|)−1 per-row penalty, so power is worse than a gap-conditional test, and the alternative must be ergodic on the augmented space.

What is *not* covered, and what a gap-aware test would need to prove itself:
* **Gap-conditional likelihood** L_t^{gap} = inf_{P∈𝒫} Σ_{(x,g)} N_{x,g} KL(Q̂(x,g,·) ‖ P^g(x,·)) with rows indexed by (x,g) and a null that **ties rows across g through the single P**. The pathwise martingale/mixture argument behind Alg 1 (Remark 4.2) is row-wise and plausibly transfers when g_i is predictable (F_{i−1}-measurable) or independent of the future chain, but this is a conjecture, not a theorem in the paper. Constants (β_t with m−1 per row) and the lower bound (Thm 3.3 uses the stationary weights of the 1-step chain) would need re-derivation.
* **Informative observation mechanism** (gap or whether-observed depends on the chain's latent evolution, or a decision to query depends on outcomes): then Y_i | past, g_i is not distributed as P^{g_i}; neither Sethi nor the thinning reduction applies. Requires an explicit model of the observation mechanism or a design (randomised, logged propensity) that restores predictability.
* **Gap-dependent efficiency claims.** Optimality is for the 1-step chain; Thm 3.3 does not describe the best achievable τ under a gap law.
* **Estimated vs known P^g.** P^g is a deterministic function of P, so no extra parameters — but the nonconvex image set matters for the inner optimisation.

Consequences for Q1: (i) exact-gap evidence is **not** subsumed — it is a genuinely new object relative to Sethi; (ii) its validity must be proved or stress-tested separately (state filtration, predictability of g_i, null class); (iii) the correct published comparators are: Sethi on thinned data (constant g), Sethi with augmentation (iid gaps), ShiftLab Markov-LR (`atlas_dependence_baselines`), and ShiftLab Gap-Mix (`origin/research/gave-r-2026-11-06:src/shiftlab/gaver/gapmix.py`, its exact-gap/envelope construction, development-only); (iv) closing the gap needs a prior-art check beyond this one paper (WP4) — failure to find it here is not novelty.
