## Decision rule

Comparators are ranked by match to the estimand and observable feedback, not by citation count. A method enters a quantitative head-to-head only when its input information, decision unit, target quantity, event ordering, and resource accounting can be made valid on the same task. If these differ, retain the method as an endpoint-specific or scope comparator and do not pool alarm/promotion rates into a common leaderboard. An adapted implementation is labeled as an adaptation, not a paper reproduction.

**High-priority scope comparator:** Digalakis et al., *The Challenger* (arXiv:2512.18390v2), already studies sequential incumbent–challenger switching using paired, potentially monetized performance gaps, explicit data/retraining/operating/switching costs, finite-epoch confidence bounds, and regret against a full-foresight oracle. It is not a drop-in quantitative comparator for the current benchmark because its central contract is a challenger trained on gradually arriving new features and a learning-curve switching objective. The full source-level comparison and non-reproduction decision are recorded in [`challenger_switching_fidelity_audit.md`](challenger_switching_fidelity_audit.md). No ShiftGuard novelty claim should treat the general evidence-to-costed-switch transition as open.

## Scope-only or task-conditional comparators

These remain in related work and the fidelity matrix, but are **not required in the current common-label benchmark** unless the manuscript adds their task or estimand:

- **WATCH and Monitoring Risks in Test-Time Adaptation:** WATCH requires its conformal/calibration setting; the TTA monitor requires continuously updated models, source calibration, a compatible loss proxy, and its proxy-separation condition. Both target predictive/distributional endpoints rather than paired decision-cost harm. No valid mapping to paired harm has been established. The TTA source/code and task-fit audit is in [`tta_monitor_fidelity_audit.md`](tta_monitor_fidelity_audit.md).
- **Decision-Focused Evaluation of Worst-Case Distribution Shift (Ren, Byun, and Wilder, UAI 2024):** an essential scope comparator for downstream resource-allocation harm. It finds worst-case shifts aligned to the allocation objective, but does not perform sequential monitoring or certify interventions. The current scalar-policy generator does not reproduce its hierarchical, combinatorial allocation contract; see [`decision-costed shift audit`](decision_costed_shift_prior_work_audit_v1.md).
- **Decision-Making Under Selective Labels (Wei, ICML 2021):** an essential Q3 scope comparator and a candidate baseline only for IID accept/reject tasks where action controls label observation and discounted reward is the objective. ShiftLab's independent post-decision label query and sequential paired-harm gate are different contracts; do not compare their utilities without a matched implementation. See [`decision-costed shift audit`](decision_costed_shift_prior_work_audit_v1.md).
- **Modeling and Controlling Deployment Reliability under Temporal Distribution Shift (Rahman & Tabassum, arXiv:2604.02351v1):** mandatory Q3 scope comparator for drift-triggered, cost-aware reliability maintenance and cost–volatility frontiers. It uses annual fully labeled credit-risk cohorts, AUC/ECE-volatility endpoints, and an imposed intervention-cost scale; its threshold sweep/knee uses the full evaluation horizon. Current ShiftGuard tasks do not provide a fair head-to-head contract. See [`source/task audit`](temporal_reliability_cost_control_audit.md).
- **The Challenger (Digalakis et al., 2026 preprint):** mandatory scope comparator for cost-aware sequential challenger switching. It is not quantitatively included in the current cross-track frontier because the present data lack its gradually arriving new-feature/training-learning-curve contract. Do not claim a generic evidence-to-switching contribution without a faithful reproduction or a demonstrably different guarantee/estimand. See [`fidelity audit`](challenger_switching_fidelity_audit.md).
- **AnyRank and e-LOND:** relevant if the paper explicitly studies multi-policy ranking or a continuing discovery stream. AnyRank's current star-shaped pilot is not a full ranking reproduction; its strict distribution-ordering target differs from mean cost improvement.
- **HCPI, CPC, CPS, and RCPS:** require their policy/trajectory, safe-reference or set-valued action, treatment-support and calibration contracts. Current common-label classification tasks do not identify the necessary action-dependent outcomes.
- **CABAI:** requires separate arm-specific experimentation and implementation costs; the shared-label task reveals all deterministic candidate costs from one outcome and does not match its allocation problem.
- **CUVET:** its documented allocation math is implemented, but the audited release fails the source response-shape conditions and lacks a verified assignment contract. Do not force a policy-value comparison on it.
- **R-IDeA and other adaptive treatment allocation methods:** require a defensible assignment/propensity and downstream causal estimand. Current candidate sources are not cleared for that inference.
- **Active sequential two-sample testing and MMD/CUSUM:** may appear in the distribution-change panel only; they are not downstream-harm comparators.

The individual applicability decisions and the evidence behind them are retained in [`comparator_applicability_audit_v1.md`](comparator_applicability_audit_v1.md). Exclusion here means “not a valid comparator for the declared common task under current information,” not “inferior method.”

## What this closes and what remains open

This document records a ranked inclusion/exclusion decision, but the Q2 logged-policy comparator set is now **reopened** after identifying Wang (2026), a directly relevant sequential improvement-certification preprint. Its one-step contextual fit and supplementary implementation have not yet been assessed. The decision is therefore not closed for Q2. It does not close paper-to-code fidelity gaps, convert development adaptations into reproductions, or establish one cross-track numerical frontier. Q1, Q2, and Q3 target different quantities and feedback contracts, so their metrics must remain in separate panels.

The manuscript's working scope is now selected: a retrospective benchmark and failure analysis across Q1–Q3, with separate estimands and explicit real-data limitations. The research objective itself remains broader than any claim currently supported; do not imply that the selected paper framing makes the full integrated system empirically complete.

The remaining research work is:

1. Finish the source-by-source literature review and verify whether the benchmark/failure-analysis contribution is differentiated enough to publish. If not, stop or pivot rather than state an unsupported novelty claim.
2. Verify that the completed and planned real-data studies satisfy the charter's actual-monitoring, candidate-selection, intervention/resource, timing, and independent-evaluation requirements. EIA and Upworthy are component-level evidence only; neither supports an end-to-end real-world intervention claim.
3. Complete the claim-to-evidence reconciliation, figure/table review, final artifact verification, and manuscript/code freeze. Any additional experiment must have its own registered question, eligible comparator, primary metric, resource budget, and stopping rule.

The frozen reference methods and current analysis frontier are recorded separately in the [reference implementation manifest](reference_implementations_v1.json) and [decision gate report](decision_gate_report_2026-10-26.md). No new experiment is authorized by this scope decision alone.
