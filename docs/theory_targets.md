# DIVE theory targets

These are open proof targets. The current implementation establishes none of the unresolved claims by code or simulation.

1. **Sequential validity with covariate-adaptive querying.** State a filtration in which `X_t` is revealed before the query randomization and `Y_t` afterward. Prove that prediction-powered corrected decision contrasts preserve the conditional-mean null and the betting-process supermartingale property when `q_t` and the bounded proxy are predictable from the revealed covariate and prior history. Clarify whether the target null is conditional on past history only or pointwise in current `X_t`.
2. **Delayed feedback.** Define arrival-time updates with event-time predictable propensities, and show whether event-order estimates can be processed on label arrival without invalidating the test. Account for pending labels at stopping and nonrandom delay.
3. **Adaptive acquisition value.** Derive an allocation criterion for reducing uncertainty in the decision-harm estimand or probability of a valid escalation, including heteroskedastic residuals, heterogeneous query costs, floors/caps, and a fixed total label budget. Compare it with Neyman allocation and EVSI/EVPI-style acquisition. The standard cost-constrained variance optimum `q ∝ sqrt(v/k)` is implemented and was mixed empirically: it improved stream-estimation RMSE over variance-only querying in five of six synthetic scenarios, but did not improve harm detection. A cost-neutral propensity branch `q_0(k)=b/k` and its convex-mixture expected-spend drift bound are integrated into the monitor and unit-tested. A 30-seed pilot verified spend stability but did not improve harm detection over uniform querying. A four-value CUDA tolerance grid passed all expected-spend checks but was exploratory (10 paired seeds); see `outputs/cost_tolerance_grid_exploratory/reports/tolerance_grid.md`. In a fresh 60-seed matched-bound hard-budget study (common `q_min=0.10`, 20% target spend), cost-stabilized allocation detected 12/60 boundary-harm streams versus 8/60 for uniform (paired difference `+0.067`, 95% seed-bootstrap CI `[0.000, 0.150]`), with similar RMSE and about 44 more labels. The interval touches zero and is unadjusted across methods; this is not an established benefit. All five methods had 0/60 false escalations in stationary and benign-nuisance streams; exact 95% upper bound is 0.060. See `outputs/hard_budget_matched_qmin_010_replication_60/reports/matched_bound_replication_analysis.md`. The next step is power analysis and a fresh evaluation block only if the candidate benefit remains plausible; do not claim a general optimum. A theoretically justified eventwise e-process bound remains a separate possibility, but would require conditioning on current covariates, not just past history.

   An additional exploratory policy, `evidence_growth_proxy`, solves a constrained KKT allocation for the pre-monitor model's stake-weighted expected log-wealth increment. The per-event objective is concave when betting factors are positive; `q(x)` is clipped to stated propensity bounds and a dual multiplier matches expected validation label spend. This is a global optimum only for that model-based surrogate, not the exact log-mixture wealth, realized alternative, or detection power. A fresh 30-seed CUDA block found that the raw rule's mean propensity rose to 13.9% under benign nuisance shift versus its 10% validation rate, with no boundary-harm detections (0/30 versus 1/30 for uniform PPI). A uniform-mixture rate-stabilized variant with a predeclared 0.01 rate-drift tolerance held mean propensity near 10%, but also detected 0/30 boundary-harm streams versus 3/30 for uniform (paired rate difference -0.10; 95% paired bootstrap interval [-0.233, 0.000]). The interval is unadjusted and the event count is small; the present evidence does not justify using this allocation as a primary method. See `outputs/evidence_growth_stabilized_replication_30/reports/evidence_growth_paired_comparison.md`. This objective does not change the validity theorem, which still depends on predictable propensities and the global PPI bound.
4. **Candidate promotion.** Construct an independent or cross-fitted evaluation protocol for repeated shadow candidates, with simultaneous or online error control, explicit deployment costs, and rollback. Distinguish improvement against a fixed reference from counterfactual policy-value estimation.

   A finite candidate-grid benchmark compares validation-rank spending, equal spending, and validation-only power weights for sequential and empirical-Bernstein gates. Each candidate weight is fixed before independent shadow feedback; the finite-family sum is checked against the familywise level. In 30 CUDA seeds, rank-spending empirical Bernstein had higher promotion rates than equal spending under boundary-local harm (30/30 versus 25/30) and recurring regimes (30/30 versus 22/30), with paired expected-net-gain differences of 42.5 (95% CI 0.2–84.9) and 65.3 (14.5–116.1). Validation-power weights did not improve over rank spending, and pilot-weighted multiple testing is established prior work. Exact-copy controls had 0/180 certificates under each allocation/gate combination; this is a diagnostic, not the proof of familywise validity. See `outputs/promotion_grid_alpha_final/` and `docs/methods_and_assumptions.md`.

   The timing comparison is implemented in `outputs/promotion_timing_v6/`. Across 1,000 independent CUDA streams per schedule, both sequential processes had 32/1,000 promotions under the no-benefit null (3.2%, Wilson 95% CI 2.3–4.5%). In the late-onset persistent regime, the three-start mixture promoted 998/1,000 versus 688/1,000 for the full-start process and 520/1,000 for empirical Bernstein; mean oracle post-promotion net gains were 212.6 (95% t CI 211.5–213.8), 120.5 (115.4–125.7), and 74.9 (70.4–79.3), respectively. In the benefit-reversal scenario, full-start and three-start processes promoted 997/1,000 and 996/1,000 candidates after an early positive phase had already triggered, and mean oracle future net gains were −30.1 (−30.8 to −29.4) and −35.2 (−36.0 to −34.4). Both fixed-horizon gates promoted 1/1,000 and avoided most of that reversal, at the cost of waiting until the fixed horizon. These are controlled synthetic outcomes, not evidence that fixed-horizon testing is generally preferable. The e-process null is eventwise; rejecting it during a positive phase does not certify that future benefit persists. The multi-start and fixed-horizon procedures are established baselines, so no novelty claim follows.

   A two-phase intersection-union gate is now implemented in `outputs/persistence_promotion_grid/`. It uses disjoint fixed discovery and persistence windows and tests the union null that at least one phase has nonpositive eventwise conditional gain. Each phase e-process uses level 0.05; conjunctive rejection controls the union null at 0.05 without an alpha split. In 1,000 CUDA streams, the 0.50 discovery split reduced benefit-reversal promotions to 6/1,000 (0.6%, 95% Wilson interval 0.3–1.3%) from 997/1,000 for the full-start e-process. Mean oracle future net gain was still negative (−0.372 [−0.669, −0.075]), while never promoting yielded zero. For persistent benefit, the same split promoted 974/1,000 and returned mean expected net gain 155.95 [153.89, 158.02], below the full-start process's 255.73 [254.31, 257.15]. The 0.75 split promoted only 42/1,000 in the late-onset scenario, versus 998/1,000 for the multi-start process. The standard intersection-union construction is not novel; the current rule did not dominate the utility frontier.

   The next falsifiable target is now implemented as a predeclared cost-aware robustness grid in `outputs/promotion_robustness_grid_corrected/`: six evaluator-only benefit schedules (no benefit, persistent benefit, and four reversal schedules at early/short, early/long, middle/long, and late/short), four one-time switching charges (0, 10, 30, 60), and paired comparisons of full-start, three-start, fixed-horizon, persistence-certified, and never-promote rules, with 1,000 CUDA streams per schedule. No-benefit full-start promotion fell from 34/1,000 at zero cost to 2/1,000 at cost 60. Under the early long reversal at zero cost, full-start promoted 1,000/1,000 and averaged −18.01 future net value (95% t CI −18.70 to −17.31), while the persistence splits promoted only 7–8/1,000. Yet under the middle long reversal, the 0.25 split promoted all streams and had 22.8% nonpositive post-promotion net outcomes; a later reversal can arrive after confirmation. At charge 30 under persistent benefit, the 0.50 persistence split promoted 61.4% with mean net value 72.85, versus 98.8% and 193.19 for full-start. The late short reversal also shows that a 0.75 discovery split can miss a beneficial opportunity. Each charge level reuses the same generated potential outcomes and includes the charge in the tested gain and post-promotion net utility. The complete grid is reported without selecting a discovery fraction on evaluation data; intervals are descriptive and not multiplicity-adjusted. These results show a strong safety/opportunity-cost trade-off and no uniformly best split or new-algorithm claim. Repeated-candidate alpha allocation and matched label budgets remain open requirements. A future-value rule may only be pursued if validation-only selection predicts a reproducible held-out net-utility gain.
5. **Controller guarantees.** Define a finite-state or finite-action controller whose resource costs and hard statistical constraints are explicit. Any utility guarantee must account for actions changing the data, policies, query rate, and future evidence.

Do not claim a result until assumptions, theorem, proof, and a falsification-oriented simulation are all documented. First resolve whether a useful contribution remains after the closest competitors listed in `references/closest_competitors.md`.

## Decision-local predictable betting bounds

**Construction:** Normalize each candidate's direct-IPW or prediction-powered increment by its exact eventwise support bound, computed from `X_t`, its frozen policy action, the two possible outcome costs, the predictable query propensity, the prequential proxy, and amortized switching cost. This decision-aware scale can be smaller when a candidate's action contrast is locally small.

**Known argument:** Under a pointwise null conditional on both prior history and current covariates, randomized querying with a known positive propensity yields an unbiased paired-gain estimate conditional on those covariates. A predictable positive bound and fixed stake in `[0,1]` produce a nonnegative one-step supermartingale factor. Fixed mixtures and validation-frozen familywise alpha allocations preserve the standard anytime and finite-family control. This is standard betting/e-process reasoning, not a novel proof.

**Initial evidence:** A CUDA grid used 2,000 independent streams per cell, one fixed candidate, 600-event horizons, three label caps, three heterogeneous cost ratios, and one nondegenerate conditional null plus two pointwise alternatives. Under the pointwise null, maximum observed crossing was 44/2,000 (2.2%) for local bounds and 46/2,000 (2.3%) for global bounds, below 5%; this supports implementation sanity but is not the proof. At cost ratio 5 and a 30% cap, the `p=0.56` pointwise alternative crossed 415/2,000 times locally versus 280/2,000 globally, a matched difference of +6.75 points (95% t interval +5.16 to +8.34). At 20% cap the difference was +3.45 points (+2.00 to +4.90). The methods were identical when cost ratio was 1. All 27 intervals are unadjusted, so this is an exploratory signal, not confirmed superiority. Crucially, rejecting the pointwise null is not evidence of positive aggregate mean net gain. When embedded in the 13-candidate hard-budget promotion grid, the local-bound mode had zero crossings in all 15,120 stream-policy rows, despite its mean eventwise bound being 6.9% of the global scale. See `outputs/decision_local_bound_calibration/`, `outputs/budgeted_promotion_grid_local_bound/`, and the methods report.

**Open issue:** The global mode tests a history-conditional average-gain null; decision-local scaling only tests the pointwise conditional null and cannot certify aggregate benefit when subgroup gains have mixed signs. Its zero-crossing result in the candidate grid makes it unsuitable as the current promotion gate. If retained for localized diagnosis, compare it with the predictable plug-in empirical-Bernstein confidence sequences of [Waudby-Smith and Ramdas](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/86/1/1/7043257) and direct paired-loss monitoring at matched error and label budgets. The next mathematical route should preserve the aggregate estimand while exploiting predictable conditional variance or stratified evidence with an explicit mixture/weighting argument. A mixed-sign average-null simulation now verifies the estimand mismatch empirically: when equally likely strata have conditional gains +0.12 and -0.12, aggregate mean gain is exactly zero, yet decision-local crossings ranged from 2.45% to 5.75% across cost ratios 2 and 5 and query caps 10%, 20%, and 30% (2,000 streams per cell). The global-bound crossing rate ranged from 1.2% to 2.05%, below the nominal 5% in this finite calibration. This supports the distinction but is not a replacement for the conditional-expectation proof. See `outputs/mixed_mean_null_local_bound/`. Exact-copy controls remain only a code-path diagnostic.

## Repeated-candidate hard-budget result

A 60-seed CUDA experiment adds 13 repeated candidates under shared 10% and 20% pathwise label caps, comparing direct IPW and prediction-powered e-processes under uniform, variance, and decision-value querying. All seven scenario families had no certificates at the 10% cap. At 20%, prediction-powered monitoring still had none; direct IPW with rank spending promoted 18.3–21.7% of boundary-local streams and 23.3–28.3% of recurring-regime streams, with no certificates in the exact-copy family or other controls. Rank allocation improved paired expected net gain over equal allocation in those alternatives, while query-policy contrasts were mixed. This isolates a useful engineering result—bound choice can dominate power at scarce labels—but does not establish a better query policy or a new method. The intervals are descriptive and the 77 contrast families are unadjusted. The next experiment should be a locked confirmation of a specific boundary/recurring alternative against a stronger variance-adaptive confidence sequence or empirically sharpened valid bound, with explicit power and familywise-error targets. It should retain a fresh exact-copy global-null family and use independent seeds. Do not infer false-promotion control from the zero-event diagnostic alone. See `outputs/budgeted_promotion_grid_ipw/` and `docs/methods_and_assumptions.md`.

## Predictable stake calibration

The globally bounded aggregate-mean null admits predictable variance- and mean-adaptive stakes without dividing each observation by a current-covariate-specific bound. `PredictableVarianceAdaptiveEProcess` uses a smoothed plug-in Kelly stake; its validity follows from the supermartingale condition for any predictable stake in `[0,1]`. The strategy is mathematically valid but belongs to an established predictable betting family, so it is a baseline and not a novelty claim. In a 27-cell CUDA grid with 2,000 streams per cell, the aggregate-null crossing rates were 1.0–2.6% for the fixed mixture and 1.2–3.05% for the adaptive stake. Broad-positive alternatives had mean crossing rates 11.77% and 11.89%; high-cost-only alternatives had 6.07% and 6.63%. Paired cells were mixed. A direct matched grid now includes the published PM-EB construction. In 2,000 CUDA streams per cell, PM-EB crossed in 0–0.85% of aggregate-null streams, versus 1.15–2.60% for fixed mixture and 1.55–2.65% for plug-in Kelly. On broad positive alternatives, mean PM-EB crossing was 5.19% versus 11.49% for fixed mixture; on high-cost-only positive alternatives, 2.46% versus 6.13%. The Kelly rule was near the fixed mixture on average but varied by cell. These are unadjusted descriptive comparisons, not dominance claims. The next task is a locked confirmation after calibrating stakes on separate pilot seeds, and a comparison with the paper's hedged process; retain the global bound and evaluate both false-promotion control and net value. Results are in `outputs/predictable_betting_comparison/`.
