# Research log

2026-10-19: Implemented first shared CPU baseline suite. No new method proposed. The initial three-seed grid is exploratory; failures of a baseline or a proposed hypothesis require fresh replicated confirmation.

2026-10-24: Expanded the delayed-feedback benchmark with bounded-mean full-stream betting and a prespecified eight-start convex mixture, then calibrated both alongside the Hoeffding process on 2,000 independent stationary and benign-variance streams per query-rate cell. All exact two-sided 95% null upper limits were at most 2.14%. The preregistered late-onset, weak-harm alternative detected 933/2,000 streams with the restart mixture versus 100/2,000 with full-stream betting; a second disjoint 2,000-seed block reproduced 895/2,000 versus 100/2,000. A 12-cell factorial with 2,000 paired seeds per cell found positive restart-mixture detection gains in every cell (1.2–68.4 percentage points), with all exact paired tests significant after Holm adjustment. This establishes a strong simulator-specific baseline result, not a novel method or real-data guarantee; delayed-label informativeness, serial dependence, policy adaptation, and causal decision utility remain untested. The protocols, row-level metrics and hashes are in docs/delayed_null_calibration_protocol.md and outputs/delayed_null_calibration_2000, outputs/delayed_feedback_confirmation_2000, outputs/delayed_feedback_replication_2000, and outputs/delayed_feedback_grid_confirmation_2000.

2026-10-24: Ran the frozen matched-path support-bound ablation under the same delayed Markov process. With exogenous precommitted query schedules, fixed and eventwise systems had identical requested labels and evidence horizons, isolating the bound. Constant q=0.20 gave exactly identical outcomes. Across periodic q schedules {0.05,0.35}, {0.05,0.55}, and {0.05,0.80}, eventwise-minus-fixed macro detection gains were +7.08, +4.58, and +7.50 points (60 paired seeds; descriptive seed-bootstrap 95% intervals [+3.33,+11.25], [+1.25,+8.75], [+3.75,+11.67]). 2026-10-24: Screened predictable convex reweighting of the existing five bounded stakes on matched eventwise-bound, periodic-query, delay-12 paths. The 60-seed development grid nominated eta=0 (uniform per-event rebalancing) over the eta=1 fixed mixture: 27/240 versus 15/240 detections, paired macro difference +5.00 pp (95% seed-bootstrap CI [+2.50,+7.92]); all four dose point estimates favored the candidate. This winner is not history-adaptive because eta=0 fixes weights uniformly at each event. On the frozen independent 2,000-stream-per-cell calibration, candidate exact-null alarms were 88/2,000 (4.40%; Bonferroni simultaneous upper 5.54%), so it failed the preregistered <=5% upper-limit gate. The fixed mixture had 48/2,000 (2.40%; upper 3.29%); both methods passed the strict-negative control cell and had no support/budget violations. The disjoint harm confirmation was correctly not run. This is a failed advancement, not evidence that the candidate's true type-I rate exceeds 5%, and its development power is not confirmatory. The predictable convex-factor argument is standard and no novelty is claimed. 2026-10-27: Closed G0 and ran G1 for the anchored, gated monitor protocol. G0 passed narrowly: DISCERN Appendix A, PPE, and Xu et al. Sections 2–3 do not use a support-boundary anchor to make the one-sided stake range independent of the label probability (recorded in the novelty audit). G1, an exact learner-side expected-log-growth calculation on train/validation slices of seeds 58000000–58000019 (no monitor runs), **failed**: at the registered 20% label-dollar budget the candidate tied DISCERN-style gating exactly (ratio 1.000; mean G* 0.02763 vs A1 0.00367). The two policies disagree on only 8.9% of events, so gated arms label every disagreement, and the anchor has no effect. In declared secondary budgets (2–10%, where q_D < 1), the anchor added at most 1.23× over gating (ratio of means; max seed 1.28×), below the 1.5× criterion. Stop rule S2 fires and the ADG algorithm branch stops. Main carried-forward finding: the repository's ungated sparse-label monitors spend about 91% of labels on known-zero events, and gating is predicted to give 5–18× higher log-growth at matched spend. That needs empirical verification before use in the benchmark manuscript. Commands, hashes, and limits are in `docs/adg_growth_prescreen_results.md`. Assessment: stop ADG; pivot options (an empirical gated-vs-ungated comparison; a standalone e-detector baseline) require new approved protocols.
