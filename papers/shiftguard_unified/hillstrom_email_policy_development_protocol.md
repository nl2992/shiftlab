# Hillstrom randomized email allocation: development protocol

**Protocol status:** exploratory development protocol, documented after the first execution. The initial 50/20/30 split has already been evaluated and is exposed. No result from this source will be described as an untouched confirmatory holdout. The protocol records the task contract for reproducibility; it is not a preregistration.

## Source and provenance

The source is the 64,000-customer MineThatData E-Mail Analytics Challenge dataset. The publisher states that approximately one third of customers were randomly assigned to each of a men's email, women's email, or no-email control group; customer attributes precede the campaign, and visit, conversion, and spend were measured over the following two weeks. The official challenge page and download link are cited in the [real-data inventory](real_data_inventory.md). The downloaded CSV is 3,964,977 bytes, SHA-256 `0e5893329d8b93cefecc571777672028290ab69865718020c78c7284f291aece`, and is excluded from Git under `data/raw/`.

The publisher offered the file for a research challenge and explicitly encouraged students and data-mining researchers to analyze it. No explicit modern license was identified. The raw file remains local and is not redistributed; generated artifacts contain aggregate metrics only. The official download endpoint was HTTP, so the recorded hash provides local reproducibility but is not a publisher-signed authenticity proof.

## Decision and target

The intervention is assigning the Women's email versus assigning no email. The men's-email arm is not a candidate action; its records are retained in the randomized population and contribute zero to this two-action Horvitz–Thompson score. The target policy may use only the eight publisher-described pre-campaign features: `recency`, `history_segment`, `history`, `mens`, `womens`, `zip_code`, `newbie`, and `channel`. Post-assignment outcomes and treatment labels are evaluator-only for policy-value estimation. `exposure` is not present in this source and is not used.

The publisher's challenge asks which customers to target under a 10,000-customer mailing capacity, so the capacity is fixed at `10,000 / 64,000 = 0.15625` of the population. The observed two-week `spend` in USD is the primary outcome; `conversion` and `visit` are secondary. Email costs are not supplied. The cost grid `$0, $0.05, $0.10, $0.25, $0.50, $1.00` per email is imposed sensitivity analysis, not observed campaign cost, profit, or operational utility.

For policy probability `π(x)` in `{0,1}` (or a stochastic policy in `[0,1]`), estimate value with the publisher-described one-third assignment probabilities:

`φᵢ(π) = 3·1{Tᵢ = Women's}·π(Xᵢ)·Yᵢ + 3·1{Tᵢ = No Email}·(1−π(Xᵢ))·Yᵢ`.

Net value subtracts `κ·π(Xᵢ)` for imposed email cost `κ`. Report the individual-policy estimates and paired row-level differences. Wald intervals use the empirical standard deviation of paired influence scores divided by the square root of evaluation rows; they assume independent customers and the stated randomized assignment design.

## Split and policy methods

Use a row-index permutation generated by `numpy.random.default_rng(seed=20261026)`, with 50% training, 20% development, and 30% evaluation. Fit preprocessing on training features only. Fit separate spend regressions for the Women's-email and no-email arms; spend standardization uses only training outcomes from those two policy arms. Select model stopping epoch or Ridge regularization using development outcomes only. The men's-email outcome labels are not used to train, scale, or select the Women's-email-vs-control policy. No evaluation outcomes are inputs to fitting, ranking, or cost selection.

Compare no email, email all, stochastic random allocation at the fixed capacity, a historical-spend top-capacity rule, a neural two-model T-learner, and a Ridge two-model T-learner. Learned policies rank predicted incremental spend and select the top capacity fraction. The Ridge penalty is chosen from `{0.1, 1, 10, 100}` on the development split. Neural model training uses PyTorch CUDA when available, with a CPU fallback.

The main diagnostic contrast is the neural T-learner top-capacity policy versus random allocation at an imposed cost of `$0.25` per email. This contrast was encoded in the first runner before that run, but this document was written after the run; treat all results as exploratory. The other costs and policy comparisons form descriptive sensitivity analyses without confirmatory multiplicity claims.

## Interpretation boundary

This is one retrospective, fixed-horizon randomized intervention study. It does not test distribution-shift monitoring, sequential evidence, feedback delay, or verified online label-release time. It does not validate future campaign benefit. The two-week outcome window is not an individual release clock. Results apply to this historical sample and imposed cost scenarios only. The 2026-10-26 evaluation split is now exposed; any new model choice on it is development, and a new independent dataset is required for final confirmation.
