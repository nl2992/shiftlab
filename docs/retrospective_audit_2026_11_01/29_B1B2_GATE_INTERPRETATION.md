# B1/B2 10% gate and continuous effects

The B1 and B2 Q1 protocols require a cost reduction of at least 10% against the selected deployable BEST comparator in **every** budget/switch-cost setting, a paired interval excluding zero in every setting, and no world more than 10% worse than BEST within that world. A nonpositive difference in at least half the settings would be NEGATIVE; otherwise failure of the conjunction is MIXED. B2 is a development round motivated by B1's post hoc diagnostic, so a B2 effect is not independent confirmation.

`scripts/retrospective_audit/audit_b1b2_gates.py` recomputes each setting's equally weighted four-world mean, relative reduction, world-level deviation, stored gate flags and Q1 decision from the archived `summary.json` cells. `B1B2_GATE_RECONCILIATION.json` records all eight rows. B1's four reductions are 3.347%, 3.502%, 0.143%, and 1.066%; none passes the 10% condition. B2's four are 10.202%, 9.645%, 7.877%, and 7.673%. B200_cs5 passes all three registered conditions. B200_cs20 misses 10% by 0.355 percentage points. B400_cs20 misses the 10% average rule and has a 14.286% within-world worsening (W3), above the 10% cap. Both historical Q1 verdicts remain **MIXED**, not a new positive confirmation or proof of zero effect.

B2's raw 500-seed cost and fixed-selected-comparator bootstrap arithmetic were separately checked in `B2_RECOMPUTATION.json`. The B1 audit here is summary-cell arithmetic; its stored paired intervals are inherited. BEST is selected by observed evaluation-seed means from P0/P1/P2 (or P2b where applicable), so the interval against that fixed selected comparator does not account for comparator selection. P5 is an oracle reference and was excluded from deployable BEST. All effects are for the imposed synthetic four-world average; practical switching value and other distributions need separate analysis. No old protocol or result was edited.
