# Next research phase: decision-harm evidence efficiency

**Status:** Updated after the 30-seed harm severity/onset/horizon grid completed. This is an experiment sequence, not a claim that the repository's three paper tracks are complete.

## Decision from the latest study

Do not advance interval-robust mixture-growth allocation as the lead method. In the independent 60-seed boundary-local study it detected 0/60 harmful streams, versus 4/60 for uniform and 0/60 for exact mixture-growth. It reduced estimator RMSE relative to simple heuristics but not relative to exact mixture-growth, and it processed a shorter stream than uniform. Keep it as a tested comparator. See `docs/robust_growth_grid_protocol.md` and `outputs/robust_growth_grid_fresh_60/`.

## Phase A: lock the estimand and monitor benchmark

1. Keep the primary endpoint as paired deployed-minus-reference decision cost, with a fixed materiality margin and explicit population null. Separate discrepancy, predictive degradation, and decision harm in every result.
2. Use the existing bounded conditional-mean e-processes and direct paired-loss monitor as established controls. Include exact and robust mixture-growth, uniform, variance, cost-variance, and decision-value acquisition policies.
3. Match the hard realized label-dollar cap, propensity support, common e-process increment bound, random numbers, seed streams, and observable information across every policy. Report both labels and events processed because equal dollars do not imply equal horizons.
4. Define stationary and benign nuisance scenarios as nondegenerate nulls; include local boundary harm, predictive-only degradation, variance/tail shifts, recurrence, and harmful adaptation as alternatives or stress tests. Ensure the onset is reached before budget exhaustion or report nondetection as censored.

## Phase B: separate calibration from power

The paired budget-frontier screen is complete: it compares 10%, 20%, and feasible 25% realized-dollar caps with common streams, query uniforms, and a common e-process bound. All methods respected the cap; all had 0/60 detections in each harmful scenario at each cap, even though all harmful onsets were reached. Exact and robust mixture-growth lowered stream-mean RMSE against uniform, but did not produce harm detections. The 30% target was outside the cost-neutral feasible interval under `q_max=0.80`, so it was replaced with 25% before the upper-budget results were generated. See `docs/budget_frontier_protocol.md`, `docs/budget_frontier_results.md`, and `outputs/budget_frontier_60/`.

The matched monitor diagnostic found 0/30 detections for fixed mixture, predictable variance, and PM-EB in both harmful scenarios under three acquisition policies, with all onsets reached. The paired uniform-query bound sensitivity then changed only `q_min` from 0.05 to 0.20 on the same 30 streams: fixed mixture detected 19/30 boundary-local and 20/30 recurring harms, predictable variance detected 19/30 in each, and PM-EB detected 0/30 and 1/30. Query counts, spend, and horizon matched exactly. The two null controls remained at 0/30 alarms, which motivated the frozen independent calibration protocol in `docs/null_calibration_qmin020_protocol.md`.

The calibration completed 2,000 streams in each of the stationary and benign-nuisance population-null controls. Fixed mixture, predictable variance, and PM-EB each had 0/2,000 false escalations in each scenario; the four primary/secondary cells have a Bonferroni simultaneous one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson upper limit of 0.00219. There were no cap violations. This supports a low false-escalation rate under these two simulator controls and stated assumptions; it does not establish validity under delayed feedback, serial dependence, selective labels, model misspecification, or candidate selection. See `docs/null_calibration_qmin020_protocol.md` and `outputs/null_calibration_qmin020_2000/`.

The disjoint 200-stream-per-alternative confirmation at `q_min=.20` completed with all onsets reached and no budget violations. Fixed mixture detected 145/200 boundary-local streams (72.5%) and 131/200 recurring-regime streams (65.5%); predictable variance detected 125/200 (62.5%) and 126/200 (63.0%); PM-EB detected 9/200 and 10/200. Fixed mixture had a Holm-adjusted paired advantage over variance in boundary-local harm (20 fixed-only, zero variance-only; adjusted p=3.81e-6), while the recurring-regime contrast was inconclusive (5 fixed-only, zero variance-only; adjusted p=.0625). Fixed mixture remains the primary candidate for this design; these results do not establish universal superiority or novelty. See `docs/harm_confirmation_qmin020_protocol.md` and `outputs/harm_confirmation_qmin020_200/`.

The frozen 36-cell development grid has now completed on CUDA: 1,080 stream-cell observations, 30 paired seeds per cell, with no missing material-harm cases, unreached onsets, or budget violations. Mean cellwise detection was 0.500/0.435/0.100 (fixed mixture / predictable variance / PM-EB) for boundary-local harm and 0.441/0.426/0.093 for recurring regimes. Detection increased consistently with severity, earlier onset, and horizon across the tested boundary-local slices, and with severity and horizon across recurring-regime slices. Because total label dollars scale with horizon, the horizon pattern combines time and resources. The grid is alternative-only and descriptive; false-alarm evidence comes from the separate null calibration. The [protocol](harm_power_grid_protocol.md), [results](../outputs/harm_power_grid_30/reports/harm_power_grid_results.md), [metrics](../outputs/harm_power_grid_30/metrics/harm_power_grid.parquet), [figure](../outputs/harm_power_grid_30/figures/harm_power_grid.png), and [manifest](../outputs/harm_power_grid_30/reports/manifest.json) are checked in with this update.

A subsequent fixed-dollar horizon development grid removed the horizon/resource confound. It held the generated 4,000-event streams, source-model fit, and $720 pathwise label cap fixed while making 1,000, 1,800, or 2,600 monitor events available. Across 30 seeds per cell, eventwise fixed-mixture detection was 1/30, 16/30, and 29/30 for early boundary harm; 0/30, 0/30, and 4/30 for late boundary harm; 10/30, 13/30, and 18/30 for recurring regimes; and 0/30, 2/30, and 21/30 for harmful adaptation. Predictable variance was close on most cells; its paired detection differences from fixed mixture were not significant after Holm adjustment over the 36 contrasts. Fixed mixture did outperform PM-EB and the 10% block-PPI mixture in several early-boundary, recurring, and harmful-adaptation cells. PM-EB and block PPI detected 0/30 in every harmful cell. All controls had 0/30 escalations per method/horizon, which is too small to establish calibration. The block result is consistent with the separate matched-cost comparison showing poor PPI power; it should remain a comparator. These are exploratory results, not a universal winner selection. The [frozen protocol](fixed_dollar_horizon_grid_protocol.md), [full report](../outputs/fixed_dollar_horizon_grid_development/analysis/fixed_dollar_horizon_results.md), [paired metrics](../outputs/fixed_dollar_horizon_grid_development/analysis/monitor_stream_metrics.csv), [figure](../outputs/fixed_dollar_horizon_grid_development/analysis/fixed_dollar_horizon_detection.png), and [manifest](../outputs/fixed_dollar_horizon_grid_development/analysis/analysis_manifest.json) are available.

That follow-up is now tested in a disjoint 30-seed CUDA development grid. For each horizon, an equal mixture of four valid components starting at 0, H/4, H/2, and 3H/4 was compared with the full-stream fixed mixture, predictable variance, and PM-EB using identical labels and the same $720 pathwise cap. It did not improve late boundary-harm detection: at H=2,600 it detected 0/30 streams versus 1/30 for the full-stream process, with every onset reached. It also reduced early-boundary detection (22/30 versus 29/30) and recurring-regime detection (6/30 versus 13/30). Both processes detected 23/30 harmful-adaptation streams only in the start-mixture's favor by one stream (23/30 versus 22/30), an inconclusive difference. All four monitors had zero false escalations in the 30-stream stationary and benign controls, which remain far too small for calibration. The four-start equal mixture should therefore remain a negative-result comparator, not advance as a lead procedure. Its mathematical validity follows from fixed convex mixing of valid e-processes under the same filtration and support assumptions; this standard argument does not imply power. See `docs/started_mixture_horizon_protocol.md` and `outputs/started_mixture_horizon_development/analysis/`.

The next method-development step should focus on the persistent late-onset weakness, but any weighting of start times must be fixed using data independent of the monitor outcomes (or derived from a valid predictable construction), so the supermartingale guarantee is retained. First compare plausible onset priors on development/validation streams, then freeze one weighting rule, run independent nondegenerate null calibration, and evaluate power on a disjoint block. Do not tune weights on the just-reported 30-seed grid.

The targeted closest-method audit is recorded in [`references/closest_competitors.md`](../references/closest_competitors.md). It confirms that generic deployment monitoring, active label-efficient inference, sequential distribution-shift testing, gated policy improvement, and delayed-label risk monitoring already have strong precedents. The current paired-cost monitors are specializations of established e-processes to a decision-cost estimand; the evidence does not support a general novelty claim. The event-order buffer is tested ordering and latency infrastructure, not a statistical method. The delayed-label pilot and design grid are documented in [`delayed_feedback_protocol.md`](delayed_feedback_protocol.md), [`delayed_feedback_grid_protocol.md`](delayed_feedback_grid_protocol.md), and [`outputs/delayed_feedback_grid_development/`](../outputs/delayed_feedback_grid_development/). The grid adds an eight-start equal-weight mixture of valid bounded-mean e-processes. It detects 25/30, 30/30 and 30/30 late, strong harm at query rates .10, .25 and .50; the same full-stream betting process detects 7/30, 24/30 and 30/30. For late, weaker harm, restart-mixture detection is 8/30, 16/30 and 27/30 versus 2/30, 4/30 and 5/30 for full-stream betting. At low query rate with early strong harm, the source/target risk-CS comparator detected 10/30 and full-stream betting 28/30, while the restart mixture detected 30/30. These 30-seed findings made the fixed-start mixture a promising established baseline, not a novelty result. The next mathematically grounded method screen should compare fixed start-time priors. For valid component processes E_k(t), any weights w_k fixed before the monitored stream, nonnegative and summing to one, give E_w(t)=sum_k w_k E_k(t), which remains an e-process under the same filtration. Select candidate weights only on a development block, freeze them, then evaluate on fresh null-calibration and alternative-confirmation seeds. Optimize a stated average or worst-cell detection objective rather than choosing weights from the final test block; retain equal weights as the established baseline. Any adaptive reweighting during monitoring needs its own supermartingale construction and proof.

**Update — fixed start-time prior validation:** the frozen Beta(2,2) prior was tested on disjoint CUDA seed blocks after the 30-seed development screen. Across 2,000 streams per stationary/benign-variance null cell, center-prior false escalations ranged from 0.15% to 0.85%; uniform ranged from 0.20% to 0.90%. The largest one-sided exact Clopper–Pearson upper bound was 1.62% under simultaneous 95% Bonferroni coverage over the 12 method/null cells. Across 12 paired alternative cells with 2,000 streams each, center detection averaged 80.43% versus 79.68% for uniform (+0.75 percentage points), with 236 center-only versus 55 uniform-only detections. Six cellwise exact McNemar comparisons favored center after Holm correction; other cells were unresolved or near ceiling. The worst observed cell difference was −0.05 points. A supplementary seed-cluster bootstrap interval for the macro difference was +0.60 to +0.91 points; this aggregate analysis was added after the prespecified cellwise analysis and is labeled accordingly. Keep center as a tested option, not a novel contribution or replacement for the uniform reference. Test onset/horizon mismatch and broader delay laws next. ## Phase C: compare against closest established methods

The first theorem-level audit confirms that PPRM is the closest directly relevant risk-monitoring baseline: it already combines labeled residual correction, unlabeled predictions, predictable proxy reliance, and an anytime-valid running-risk lower confidence sequence. Its source-risk estimand differs from ShiftLab's paired current-environment policy-cost contrast, and it does not certify a replacement policy. The paired-cost PPI adaptation is specified in [`pprm_paired_decision_benchmark.md`](pprm_paired_decision_benchmark.md). Its block estimator and predictable plug-in reliance rule are implemented in `src/shiftlab/dive/estimators.py`, with exact finite-law unbiasedness and formula tests. `PPIBlockConfidenceSequence` in `src/shiftlab/dive/eprocess.py` applies the CM-EB test-stream bound with a fixed finite mixture over λ; formula and exact small-null enumeration tests check the boundary and optional-look behavior. This adapts PPRM's running-risk bound to paired costs and omits its separate source-risk upper confidence bound. The adaptive-η block monitor is integrated into the hard-budget runner. The matched independent 200-stream comparison and the separate 2,000-stream registered-null calibration are complete and documented in [`pprm_paired_decision_benchmark.md`](pprm_paired_decision_benchmark.md), including the residual-variance harm reclassification. In those tested designs, block-PPI estimation can be useful, but the 10% fixed mixture did not match the eventwise monitor's harm-detection power. This is an adaptation to a new estimand, not a full reproduction or a novelty claim. Next, audit WATCH, active statistical inference, active sequential testing, and safe policy evaluation with the same detail. Each comparator must receive the same labels, filtration, policy freeze, repeated-testing budget, and false-escalation target where its assumptions permit. If the paired-cost monitor matches or exceeds the eventwise e-process under matched information and spend, report that negative result and narrow Paper 1.

## Phase D: connect monitoring to intervention

After the monitor benchmark is calibrated, run a separate frozen-candidate shadow evaluation. Do not treat detecting incumbent harm as certification of a replacement. Evaluate candidate net paired decision benefit after update, switching, and rollback costs; account for repeated candidate selection and use independent forward-time feedback. Then add a small finite-action controller for continue, query, recalibrate, adapt, retrain, or fallback, with identical information and explicit label/compute/intervention budgets.

## Exit criteria

Paper 1 advances only if one procedure demonstrates the declared false-escalation target on nondegenerate nulls and a reproducible gain in power, delay, or decision loss at matched information and cost against strong existing monitors. Paper 2 requires calibrated false promotion and positive net decision value for frozen candidates. Paper 3 requires a utility-versus-resource improvement over established cost-aware policies. Otherwise, preserve the negative result, narrow the question, or stop that contribution.

Run large screens on CUDA when generation or model fitting benefits from it, but preserve deterministic CPU smoke tests. Record requested and resolved devices, seeds, versions, code hashes, manifests, and checksums for every result.
