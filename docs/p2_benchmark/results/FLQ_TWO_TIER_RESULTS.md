## What this shows

1. **The criterion reform holds up on its own terms.** On every dependent null, front-loading
   promotes far more often than its host: for example .376 vs .104 on W_null_dep_r95, and .204 vs
   .056 on N_null_dep_r9. Yet its all-in cost is equal or lower: −1.0 [−4.0, +2.0] and −2.8
   [−4.3, −1.3] respectively. The extra promotions under dependence carried no measurable economic
   harm here. The Atlas v2 gate rejected this method on promotion rate alone.
2. **The efficacy claim does not survive new instances.** Against its host, front-loading still
   gains a lot in every region regime: −4.7 to −38.8 for the primary candidate. But it does not beat
   every certified comparator simultaneously. The blockers are `SMCS+retain-incumbent`
   (N_better_b, N_near) and `xu_acquisition_only` (N_better_a, N_better_c). On N_near the
   candidate's mean is worse than SMCS (+1.73).
3. **Reversal costs remain.** Primary vs host: +41.5 [+32.9, +50.2] on W_reversal_700 and +28.1
   [+20.0, +36.2] on N_reversal_900. It gains on V_recurrent (−14.0) and is roughly neutral on late
   onsets.
4. **Uncertified context.** `model_selector_causal` promotes on .93–.97 of the iid and dependent
   nulls (.65 on V_worse) and costs +27 to +39 vs never-switch.

## Post hoc, descriptive only (cannot change the verdict)

- `SMCS+retain-incumbent` passes Tier A too (promotion .002–.026 at n=1000). Under the reformed
  criterion it is a legitimate certified comparator. Atlas v2's dependent-null gate had excluded it
  (V_null_dep_r8 .13 there), which removed one of the strongest comparators from FLQ's original
  comparison. **The gate choice changes the leaderboard in both directions.** This is the clearest
  evidence so far for the P2 benchmark's thesis.
- Excluding the Xu composite from the comparator set would add one win (N_better_a): 3/6, still
  below 4.
- Raw dependent-null promotion rates of the comparators are similar to the candidate's. For
  example, on W_null_dep_r95: SMCS .332, Xu .240, DISCERN design floor .100, candidate .376.

## Limitations

Synthetic only. Comparators are common-contract ports, not author-native code. Empirical null rates
are not guarantees. Tier B's tolerance is measured against the host, so it does not show that
front-loading is safe in absolute terms. Both front-loading and its host cost about +6 to +10 vs
never-switch on nulls, mostly label spend.
