## Q2 comparator update — 2026-10-26

The matched MOPE/OPI synthetic comparison confirms that candidate-versus-incumbent off-policy certification and finite-class anytime policy identification are established components, not a new ShiftGuard contribution. MOPE promoted 279/300 prespecified +0.15 alternatives but also 11/1,000 null candidates and three null paths with empty confidence sets; OPI did not identify a singleton by 200 events. The estimands differ, and this single stationary IID task does not establish broad relative performance. The output and independent anomaly audit are linked from [`matched results`](opi_mope_matched_results_v1.md). The remaining possible contribution would require a verified integration of harm evidence, resource costs, and safe intervention under one valid information filtration; its real-data support and novelty remain unverified.

## Provisional conclusion

The broad idea—use sequential evidence to decide whether to continue,
collect labels, adapt, retrain, promote, or fall back—sits at the intersection
of established sequential testing, active inference, safe policy evaluation,
selective intervention, and resource-aware retraining. The fact that these strands can be written in
one action space is not, by itself, a methodological contribution.

A potentially useful contribution is a rigorously evaluated transition from
evidence to costed intervention under one explicit information filtration,
with calibration preserved across adaptively selected actions. MOPE already
establishes the candidate-certification transition for contextual-bandit
policy value, so any contribution must identify additional value beyond that
gate rather than rebrand it. The integrated
pipeline now has independently calibrated and confirmed synthetic comparisons
against simpler end-to-end arms, but shows profile-specific gains and no
general system advantage. Available real datasets still do not establish an
untouched downstream-intervention confirmation, and the source-based Q1–Q3
comparator frontier remains incomplete. The proposed contribution is still
unverified.

**Updated novelty disposition (2026-10-26):** a targeted primary-source addition now includes Wei's ICML 2021 selective-label decision policy and Ren et al.'s UAI 2024 decision-focused shift evaluation (full scope comparison in [`decision_costed_shift_prior_work_audit_v1.md`](decision_costed_shift_prior_work_audit_v1.md)). The broad unified formulation and
the transition from statistical evidence to a costed challenger/retraining
decision are already substantially represented in prior work, including
Regol et al. (2025), Digalakis et al. (2026 preprint), Molitor (2026), MOPE,
and Löfström (2026) for narrower intervention tasks. The current project has
not demonstrated that its integration adds a new guarantee, estimand, or
general empirical advantage. The only defensible current claim is a
claim-bounded comparative audit of existing methods and their failure modes
under explicit, non-interchangeable feedback contracts. Even that is a
potential empirical contribution, not yet a verified publication-level gap:
the comparable frontier is incomplete and several real studies are task
adaptations. Do not pitch “the statistically justified transition from
evidence to intervention” as novel without a direct source-by-source analysis
and matched comparison that isolates what existing methods cannot do.

**Decision:** no new algorithm is justified by the current audit. The frozen
synthetic integrated calibration and efficacy gates are complete. The method decision is now a no-go for new algorithms and a pivot to a
retrospective benchmark/failure-analysis paper, with publication viability
conditional on the finite literature/comparator closure. If the audit cannot
show a differentiated empirical result, stop/pivot rather than submit. Preserve negative and mixed results. See
[`method_decision.md`](method_decision.md) and
 [`gap_analysis.md`](gap_analysis.md).
## Q2 logged-policy certification comparators (2026-10-26)

### Instance-optimal policy-improvement certification: Wang (2026)

Wang's arXiv v1 (submitted 2026-10-17) gives an instance-dependent information lower bound and a sequential certifier for a fixed target policy versus a known, possibly history-dependent logger. Under the paper's logged-tree/environment, bounded-reward and coverage conditions, every `delta`-sound certifier must use at least `log(1/delta)/K*` expected episodes; a plug-in Kelly construction attains the exponent asymptotically in the finite-positive regime. This strengthens the prior-work boundary for broad Q2 claims about sequential certification of candidate policy improvement, and makes the logged-bandit comparator list incomplete until its task fit is assessed. The result does not directly cover shared-label predictive losses or the OBD replay without a careful mapping of the unit, logger, support, context and independence assumptions. The arXiv paper says code and raw outputs are in supplementary material, but the inspected public arXiv source archive had no implementation or separate supplement; no ShiftLab implementation or quantitative comparison exists. See [the primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24345) and the updated [`comparator applicability audit`](comparator_applicability_audit_v1.md). Treat it as an identified required comparator, not as a result reproduced by ShiftLab.

### Finite-class policy identification: Molitor (2026)

Molitor's 2026 method already supplies an anytime-valid candidate set and stopping rule for identifying the optimal policy in a finite logged contextual-bandit class, under predictable logging, known supported propensities, bounded rewards, predictable reward models, and time-invariant policy values. Therefore, “anytime-valid policy certification” alone is not a defensible novelty claim for ShiftGuard. A possible distinction would have to concern a verified integration from harm evidence to costed information acquisition and safe deployment, and would require direct matched evidence; it is not established by the present audit. OBD is a conditional offline development candidate only: slate interactions, incomplete slate IDs, feature as-of semantics, value stationarity, feedback maturity, and archive reconciliation remain unresolved. See `opi_applicability_audit_v1.md`.

The pinned author experiment code requires additional fidelity caveats: its simulation and reanalysis helpers fit OLS on the full evaluation stream, clip the full DR score, continue scoring zero-support policies via model extrapolation, and pass `alpha/m` to both one-sided bounds. These choices do not establish the stated predictable-model/support requirements and a direct union bound gives `2 alpha` across two tails and the policy class unless another argument is supplied. This narrows the implementation evidence, not a blanket conclusion about the theorem under its assumptions. ShiftLab's independent formula-level calibration used a predictable model, strict support checks, and `alpha/(2m)` per endpoint; it produced 0/1,000 false optimal eliminations under tied nulls and 80.0% unique separation under the registered unique-best alternative by horizon 2,000. The less conservative source-style tail allocation yielded 93.8% unique separation but is not treated as `alpha`-level familywise control. The run is synthetic-only; see `opi_calibration_protocol_v1.md` and `opi_applicability_audit_v1.md`.
