# P6 cost comparator and conditional-null validity

The adversarial review's archived `bl.py.txt` implements P6 as a periodic batch rule: collect `nv` released disagreement labels, compute the plain signed paired-difference mean, fire when it exceeds `thr`, and reset the count and sum after a full batch. The reported untuned arm uses `nv=25`, `thr=0.3`. It uses P3b's query pacing and the same synthetic paths for a cost comparison, but the source does not calibrate this repeated threshold to a 5% conditional-null false-promotion bound.

`scripts/retrospective_audit/audit_batch_boundary.py` gives an exact counterexample to a universal 5% claim for this fixed rule. Let every eligible disagreement label have independent paired difference +1 or -1 with equal probability, so its conditional mean is zero. If 25 labels are queried and released, the batch fires when at least 17 of them are +1. That probability is exactly `1807781/33554432 = 0.05387607216835022`, already greater than 0.05 for **one** batch. With two independent batch opportunities the probability of at least one false fire is about 0.10485. This does not measure P6's empirical null rate in the B2 generator; it shows why the archived uncalibrated P6 rule cannot be assigned P3b's anytime guarantee. `P6_BOUNDARY_COUNTEREXAMPLE.json` records the exact calculation and source hash.

The archived adversarial review reports that this P6 has 7.3–9.3% four-world cost reduction, close to P3b's 7.7–10.2%, and that P3b is cheaper in three settings and tied in one under its reported paired intervals. Those cost numbers remain **DOC-ONLY** here: the adversarial-review directory preserves source text and printed outputs but no per-seed P6 cost arrays for independent paired recomputation. Its 3×3 parameter grid was evaluated on the same seeds, so its best-in-sample version is an optimistic descriptive comparator, not a tuned deployable policy. The existing fair reevaluation already states that P6 error control was not established; this audit supplies a concrete boundary-null reason. A valid fixed-batch comparator needs a prespecified single test or a calibrated repeated-testing schedule, then matched information, cost, timing, and independent evaluation. The historical B2 MIXED gate is unchanged.

For scale, under the same *iid fair ±1* law only, the least strict threshold that keeps one 25-label opportunity below 5% is `>0.36` (fire on at least 18 positives), with false-fire probability 2.164%. If the budget allows at most eight or sixteen independent complete batches of 25 labels, `>0.52` (at least 20 positives) gives familywise probabilities 1.619% and 3.212%, respectively. The B2 budgets of 200 and 400 labels motivate those opportunity counts as upper bounds. A conservative Hoeffding-plus-union bound for bounded, conditionally mean-zero increments would instead require thresholds about 0.637 and 0.679 for eight and sixteen opportunities. These are **illustrations of calibration cost**, not validated P6 replacements: archived P6's selectively queried disagreement labels and release process have not been shown to satisfy the necessary conditional-mean assumptions, and no utility results exist for these thresholds.
