\begin{abstract}
A team holds an incumbent and a challenger model, buys delayed labels on disagreements under a hard
budget, and pays a cost to switch. Anytime-valid promotion certificates for this problem are usually
calibrated to a conditional null, which does not control the marginal null on temporally dependent
streams. We study promotion with a declared dependence class and ask what that robustness costs.
We propose \NAD, which tests the challenger against a per-step envelope on the conditional success
probability, spaces label queries by a predictable gap, and reports an anytime
dependence-robustness value. We give a validity argument for the certificate, an
information-rate characterisation of the price of robustness for a relaxed per-step class, a
two-stream design with sign-independent flagging, and a payback-certified variant.
In a registered confirmation, NAD-2S is about 3\% cheaper than the dependence-robust state of the art
(A5b, D5) on synthetic alternatives ($J(\text{NAD-2S})-J(\text{A5b})=-11.31$, 99\% CI $[-13.79,-8.84]$,
$-2.9\%$), keeps level $\alpha$ in its class (promotion $.002$ on F0\_null and F5\_dep\_null\_r8) and
passes the Tier B harm gate (max 95\% upper bound $1.40\le5$). It does \emph{not} beat A5b or D5 on 27
weakly dependent real replacement streams (ties: $+0.23$ and $+0.14$; the registered
real-stream hypotheses H2 and H3 fail), and we do not claim state-of-the-art cost: cheaper methods, namely
author-endpoint MOPE and conditional-null front-loaders, buy lower cost with marginal-null validity,
for example $.15$ pooled promotion on real marginal-null twins with 4 of 5 datasets above $\alpha$
(post-registration descriptive). On these real twins such promotions are economically harmless (mean
decision harm $\le+0.8$), which weakens the economic case for robustness on available real data.
The main contributions are the characterisation of the price of robustness and a corrected,
author-endpoint MOPE comparator. Hidden-state and heavy-tailed nulls remain outside the guarantee.
\end{abstract}
