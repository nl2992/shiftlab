# Research log

2026-10-19: Implemented first shared CPU baseline suite. No new method proposed. The initial three-seed grid is exploratory; failures of a baseline or a proposed hypothesis require fresh replicated confirmation.

2026-10-23: Ran a CUDA cost-stability tolerance sweep at δ ∈ {0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.1}, comparing the cost-stabilized proxy query policy with uniform prediction-powered querying on the same ten stream seeds in stationary, benign-nuisance, and boundary-local-harm scenarios. All 12 cells satisfied the expected-spend drift bound; the largest observed deviation was 0.0152 per event at δ=0.1. In benign nuisance, stabilized mean spend ranged from 351 to 373 versus 487 for uniform, while point RMSE was slightly higher (0.0397–0.0438 versus 0.0373); all paired 95% seed-bootstrap intervals crossed zero in this 10-seed block. At the boundary, no method detected harm in this small paired block; the separate 30-seed δ=0.05 pilot had 0/30 stabilized versus 5/30 uniform detections. Treat the latter as a descriptive pilot result, not a detection guarantee or established superiority. No new algorithm or novelty claim follows; next evaluate stronger sequential baselines at matched realized spend, with a predeclared budget and confirmatory seed block.

2026-10-23: Added a common hard label-dollar cap benchmark with a predictable reserve stopping rule and paired-seed comparison across uniform, variance, decision-value, cost-variance, and cost-stabilized query designs. On 30 CUDA seeds per scenario (450 method-stream rows), all realized spend stayed below its shared per-stream cap. In boundary-local harm, all 30 onsets were reached; uniform detected 4/30, while every adaptive design detected 0/30 under method-specific e-process bounds. A matched sensitivity rerun with common q_min=0.05 produced 0/30 detections for every method, so the initial gap is sensitive to threshold conservatism and cannot be attributed to allocation alone. Cost-stabilized querying acquired about 22 more labels than uniform at comparable realized spend, but its paired stream-mean RMSE difference was +0.0018 (95% seed-bootstrap CI [-0.0059, 0.0083]); no advantage is established. Cost-variance querying had higher RMSE than uniform by +0.0201 [0.0050, 0.0348]. The uniform process was cross-checked against the existing full-monitor run: four alarms occurred before budget stopping and were reproduced at the same times; the fifth full-monitor alarm occurred after the budget stop. The reserve stopping rule plus e-process optional stopping is a mathematically grounded baseline composition, not a novelty claim. Immediate labels and paired observable policy costs remain assumptions; no delayed-feedback or integrated-controller result is established.

2026-10-23: Ran a fresh 60-seed matched-bound hard-budget replication on CUDA after locking the protocol in `docs/hard_budget_matched_bound_protocol.md`. Because a 10% budget target made the cost-neutral branch infeasible at a common q_min of 0.10 across tariffs in [0.5, 2.5], the comparison used a 20% target and q_max=0.80; all methods shared q_min=0.10, alpha=0.05, stream seeds, and per-seed hard caps. All 900 method-stream rows passed cap validation; all 60 boundary-harm onsets were reached; false escalations were 0/60 in stationary and benign-nuisance streams (exact 95% upper bound 0.060). Cost-stabilized querying detected 12/60 boundary-harm streams versus 8/60 for uniform, paired difference +0.067 (95% seed-bootstrap CI [0.000, 0.150], unadjusted across four adaptive comparisons). RMSE was 0.0548 versus 0.0554 (paired difference -0.0006, 95% CI [-0.0082, 0.0068]), using about 44 additional labels at similar realized spend ($713 vs $712). This is a weak signal, not established superiority or a general optimum. See the full analysis in `outputs/hard_budget_matched_qmin_010_replication_60/reports/matched_bound_replication_analysis.md`. Immediate paired labels and synthetic stream assumptions remain; no delayed-feedback theorem or integrated intervention-controller result is established.
