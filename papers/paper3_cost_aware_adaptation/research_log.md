# Research log

2026-10-19: Implemented first shared CPU baseline suite. No new method proposed. The initial three-seed grid is exploratory; failures of a baseline or a proposed hypothesis require fresh replicated confirmation.

2026-10-23: Ran a CUDA cost-stability tolerance sweep at δ ∈ {0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.1}, comparing the cost-stabilized proxy query policy with uniform prediction-powered querying on the same ten stream seeds in stationary, benign-nuisance, and boundary-local-harm scenarios. All 12 cells satisfied the expected-spend drift bound; the largest observed deviation was 0.0152 per event at δ=0.1. In benign nuisance, stabilized mean spend ranged from 351 to 373 versus 487 for uniform, while point RMSE was slightly higher (0.0397–0.0438 versus 0.0373); all paired 95% seed-bootstrap intervals crossed zero in this 10-seed block. At the boundary, no method detected harm in this small paired block; the separate 30-seed δ=0.05 pilot had 0/30 stabilized versus 5/30 uniform detections. Treat the latter as a descriptive pilot result, not a detection guarantee or established superiority. No new algorithm or novelty claim follows; next evaluate stronger sequential baselines at matched realized spend, with a predeclared budget and confirmatory seed block.

2026-10-23: Added a common hard label-dollar cap benchmark with a predictable reserve stopping rule and paired-seed comparison across uniform, variance, decision-value, cost-variance, and cost-stabilized query designs. On 30 CUDA seeds per scenario (450 method-stream rows), all realized spend stayed below its shared per-stream cap. In boundary-local harm, all 30 onsets were reached; uniform detected 4/30, while every adaptive design detected 0/30 under method-specific e-process bounds. A matched sensitivity rerun with common q_min=0.05 produced 0/30 detections for every method, so the initial gap is sensitive to threshold conservatism and cannot be attributed to allocation alone. Cost-stabilized querying acquired about 22 more labels than uniform at comparable realized spend, but its paired stream-mean RMSE difference was +0.0018 (95% seed-bootstrap CI [-0.0059, 0.0083]); no advantage is established. Cost-variance querying had higher RMSE than uniform by +0.0201 [0.0050, 0.0348]. The uniform process was cross-checked against the existing full-monitor run: four alarms occurred before budget stopping and were reproduced at the same times; the fifth full-monitor alarm occurred after the budget stop. The reserve stopping rule plus e-process optional stopping is a mathematically grounded baseline composition, not a novelty claim. Immediate labels and paired observable policy costs remain assumptions; no delayed-feedback or integrated-controller result is established.

2026-10-23: Ran a fresh 60-seed matched-bound hard-budget replication on CUDA after locking the protocol in `docs/hard_budget_matched_bound_protocol.md`. Because a 10% budget target made the cost-neutral branch infeasible at a common q_min of 0.10 across tariffs in [0.5, 2.5], the comparison used a 20% target and q_max=0.80; all methods shared q_min=0.10, alpha=0.05, stream seeds, and per-seed hard caps. All 900 method-stream rows passed cap validation; all 60 boundary-harm onsets were reached; false escalations were 0/60 in stationary and benign-nuisance streams (exact 95% upper bound 0.060). Cost-stabilized querying detected 12/60 boundary-harm streams versus 8/60 for uniform, paired difference +0.067 (95% seed-bootstrap CI [0.000, 0.150], unadjusted across four adaptive comparisons). RMSE was 0.0548 versus 0.0554 (paired difference -0.0006, 95% CI [-0.0082, 0.0068]), using about 44 additional labels at similar realized spend ($713 vs $712). This is a weak signal, not established superiority or a general optimum. See the full analysis in `outputs/hard_budget_matched_qmin_010_replication_60/reports/matched_bound_replication_analysis.md`. Immediate paired labels and synthetic stream assumptions remain; no delayed-feedback theorem or integrated intervention-controller result is established.

2026-10-23: Added an exact conditional McNemar power analysis for the nominated cost-stabilized policy versus uniform. Pilot pairs were 5 candidate-only detections, 1 uniform-only detection, and 54 ties; the one-sided exact p-value against equal marginal rates was 0.1094. Under plug-in discordance assumptions, 151 fresh seeds give 80% power for the easier null of zero difference at alpha=0.05 (219 at alpha=0.0125); these optimistic planning values do not test the locked +0.10 practical margin, since the observed point effect is only +0.067. No further confirmatory GPU run is justified until the candidate is improved on development data or a paired-risk-difference design for the practical margin is specified. Details: `outputs/hard_budget_matched_qmin_010_replication_60/reports/paired_detection_power_plan.md`.

2026-10-23: Ran a fresh CUDA extension across boundary-local harm, residual-variance shift, recurring regimes, and harmful adaptation (60 seeds per scenario; 1,200 method-stream rows; five query policies; common `q_min=0.10` e-process bound; 20% hard dollar budget). All 60 harmful onsets per scenario were reached before budget stopping, and all 1,200 rows respected their per-stream hard spend caps. There were no false escalations in the 60 streams per method-scenario for this synthetic block. Detection remained low: for boundary harm the methods ranged from 3/60 to 8/60; for recurring regimes, 0/60 to 4/60; for harmful adaptation, 0/60 to 1/60; and for residual-variance shift, all methods were 0/60. This confirms that the implemented evidence process is conservative at these settings and that shift type matters; zero observed alarms in 60 seeds does not establish a zero false-alarm probability, and low power prevents claims that methods fail universally. No policy dominated estimation error across scenarios: cost-stabilized minus uniform RMSE was -0.0044 (95% seed-bootstrap CI [-0.0100, 0.0011]) for boundary harm, -0.0026 [-0.0096, 0.0045] for recurring regimes, +0.0050 [-0.0003, 0.0107] for residual variance, and -0.0014 [-0.0057, 0.0029] for harmful adaptation. These intervals are descriptive and unadjusted for method selection. The study is exploratory and uses immediate synthetic paired labels; it is not evidence of a novel test or deployment guarantee. Protocol: `docs/hard_budget_extended_scenarios_protocol.md`; metrics and paired comparisons: `outputs/hard_budget_extended_scenarios_60/reports/hard_budget_comparison.md`.

2026-10-23: Added and tested a fixed equal-weight multiscale betting mixture with 13 geometric stakes from `1/1024` to `0.99`, alongside the existing five-stake mixture `(0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.40, 0.80)`. The validity argument is unchanged: every bounded betting factor is nonnegative and has conditional expectation at most one under the specified conditional-mean null, and a fixed convex mixture preserves that property. On a fresh matched CUDA block (60 seeds per scenario, five allocation policies, four harmful mechanisms; 1,200 rows), the existing mixture detected 49 streams and the multiscale mixture 44; all five discordant pairs favored the existing mixture and none favored multiscale. A bootstrap clustered by the 60 independent seeds across the 20 scenario-policy outcomes per seed estimated a multiscale-minus-fixed detection difference of -0.42 percentage points (95% descriptive interval [-0.92, -0.08]). The broad prior's added low stakes diluted weight on the useful existing stakes and did not recover missed residual-variance detections. In matched null controls, stationary streams had 0 alarms in 60 seeds per policy for both mixtures. Benign-nuisance streams also had zero alarms per policy, but the evaluator marked 6/60 seeds as decision-harm onsets for the specified policy-reference pair, so this is not a pure null family. These results reject the unweighted broad grid as an improvement in this benchmark; they do not reject other priors or establish a general power ordering. Outputs: `outputs/hard_budget_multiscale_stakes_60/` and `outputs/hard_budget_multiscale_null_controls_validated_60/`; protocol: `docs/multiscale_betting_protocol.md`.
